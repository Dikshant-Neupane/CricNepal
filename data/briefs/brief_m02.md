# Match 2 prep brief — what Biratnagar v Chitwan tells the Janakpur Bolts

Prepared 27 Oct 2026, the afternoon of the match. Sources: ESPNcricinfo full scorecard (Match 2, verified; capture entry closes arithmetically in data/capture/), CricNepal ball-by-ball dataset for NPL Seasons 1–2 (64 matches, TU Kirtipur), player priors file.

**Result:** Chitwan Rhinos won by 8 runs. Chitwan 157/3 in 20 overs; Biratnagar 149 all out in 19.3 overs. Toss: Biratnagar, fielded. Player of the Match: Kamal Singh Airee (4/26).

## The one-line read

Chitwan posted almost exactly their historical bat-first score and defended it with a domestic seam core, because Biratnagar's chase, ahead of the game at 15 overs, lost its last five wickets for 17 runs. The lesson is not that 157 is a big score at TU. It is that wickets in clusters beat rate pressure at this ground, and Biratnagar still cannot stop losing them.

## How Chitwan approach games (S1–S2 evidence, confirmed today)

- **Bat-first is their game; chasing is not.** S1–S2: batting first they average 155.9 and win 50%; chasing they average 130.3 and win only 28.6%. Today they were put in and made 157/3, right on their bat-first base rate. Price them as a set-a-target side.
- **The powerplay was Malan, and it was off their own pattern.** Chitwan's S1–S2 powerplay batting base is modest: 38.1 runs per match, usually losing 2.13 wickets. Today: 50/0, with Dawid Malan 39 off 27 in the first six. Malan batted into the 13th over for 70 off 50 (1.40 runs per ball) and fell at 90/3. Nearly half the innings' value was one batter.
- **No death surge, just no collapse.** After Malan fell, Bopara (32 not out off 26, 1.23 per ball against a 1.255 prior: exactly himself) and Mousley (29 not out off 22) added 67 unbeaten in 7.4 overs. Three wickets in 20 overs is the whole story of the innings: Biratnagar never got into the middle order.
- **Their bowling identity flipped today.** The S1–S2 profile says powerplay wickets: 2.27 per match, 3+ in 7 of 15. Today they took one powerplay wicket (Bam, 4.3 overs) and won it late instead. Ranjit Kumar's 3-0-12-1 strangled the middle overs (economy 4.00 against a 6.83 prior), and Kamal Airee took Basir (11.5), Russell (15.5), Mathews (17.5) and Rupesh (17.6) on his way to 4/26 at 6.50 an over against an 8.03 prior.
- **They did it without their headline names.** No Sikandar Raza, no Usama Mir, no Kaleem Sana in the XI. The wickets were shared by Kamal (4), Rijan Dhakal (2), Taylor, Ranjit, Mousom Dhakal and Mousley. Do not price Chitwan's attack off who is missing.

## How Biratnagar approach games — and where today broke

- **Field-first habit, again.** They field on 80% of toss wins across S1–S2; Lamichhane won this toss and fielded. Their bowling problem today was not control, it was penetration: three wickets in 20 overs. Lamichhane went 4-0-23-0 (5.75, tighter than his 6.46 prior, but wicketless), Mathews was their best (3-0-15-1, 5.00 against a 7.13 prior), Russell leaked (4-0-39-1, two no-balls), and Ranjane's one over cost 17. Chitwan's 50/0 powerplay came against a side whose S1–S2 powerplay bowling concedes 45.1 a match but usually takes 1.75 wickets doing it. Today: the runs, no wickets.
- **The chase was ahead, then it fragmented.** Bam (26 off 14, 1.86 per ball against a 1.13 powerplay prior) and debutant Imran Sheikh (39 off 31, 1.26 against a 1.16 prior) put on 40 in 4.3 overs. At 15 overs Biratnagar were 114/4 needing 44 off 30, with Russell set. Russell delivered his part: 33 off 17, 1.94 per ball, above both his overall prior (1.33) and his death prior (1.57). It still was not enough, because Mathews stalled at the other end (14 off 16, 0.88 per ball against a 1.03 middle-overs prior) and the last five wickets fell for 17 runs in 22 balls. Positions 8 to 11 contributed 0, 0, 0 and 2.
- **This is their base rate, not bad luck.** S1–S2 chasing average: 128.6, with six all-outs in 16 matches. Today they scored 149, well above that average, and were bowled out again. Their innings do not taper; they break.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **Wickets are the currency at TU, in both directions.** Match 1 was decided by 4 powerplay wickets; Match 2 by 5 wickets for 17 runs at the death. Janakpur's two known structural gaps are exactly these phases: 2.06 powerplay wickets lost per match with the bat (worst of the sides profiled) and 1.00 powerplay wicket taken per match with the ball. Both briefs so far say the same thing: the season turns on the first six overs and on not collapsing in the last five.
- **Par discipline holds.** Two day games in, the totals are 87 all out and 157/3 chased to 149. The pre-season TU tables (first-innings mean 147.5, par in the low 150s) stand. 157 was defendable because the bowling side kept taking wickets, not because the pitch died: Biratnagar were scoring at 7.60 at the 15-over mark.

## What this changes for the Bolts

**Match 12 v Biratnagar (Tue 3 Nov, 12:30):**
1. Bat through against this attack. Biratnagar took three wickets in 20 overs today; a set Janakpur batter can bat the innings. Rotate strike off Lamichhane (tight, currently wicketless) and target the fifth-bowler overs: Russell went at 9.75 and Ranjane at 17 in his single over.
2. Defend by hunting wickets, not by protecting the rate. Their chase broke in clusters (five wickets for 17). Keep attacking fields and wicket options into overs 16–20 even if Russell is in; he scored at 1.94 per ball today and still lost.
3. Their openers start fast (40 in 4.3 today). Harmeet's powerplay spell is again the first battle; a quiet first three overs against Bam and Imran is worth more than wickets chased later.

**Match 25 v Chitwan (Fri 13 Nov, 17:00):**
1. Malan is the innings. He made 70 of 157 and his dismissal at 12.2 overs is where the innings stopped growing. The plan is early: powerplay pressure and the best spin matchup Janakpur has, before he is set.
2. Respect the domestic seam core. Kamal, Ranjit, Rijan and Taylor just defended 157 with no overseas help. Chasing against them, get ahead of the rate early: their seamers defend par by taking wickets in the second half of the innings, and a chase that drifts to 44 off 30 against them is exactly the game they won today.
3. Batting first is a real option against Chitwan. Their chase base rate is the weakest of the eight sides (130.3 average, 28.6% wins). If Janakpur post 155+, the base rates and today's evidence both favour the side defending.
