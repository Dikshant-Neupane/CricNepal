## The one-line read

A below-par total lost to a chase that was decided in one over of
batting, not by the finishers. Chitwan made 129 — well below par at TU
— and Sudurpaschim still had to work for it: they were 70/4 in the
seventh over after losing two wickets in two balls. But the game had
already been tilted by Brandon McMullen's 43 off 21, and the chase
never stalled again: Ishan Pandey, Aarif Sheikh and Sherfane Rutherford
added the remaining runs without ever needing a late assault. Chitwan's
bowling produced one genuine cluster (Ranjit Kumar's three wickets)
and one discipline problem (nine extras, six of them from Sikandar
Raza's over-spell), and a 129-run defence cannot carry both.

## How Chitwan approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first total far below their own history — and below par.**
  S1–S2: batting first Chitwan average 155.9 and win 50.0% of those
  games (8 games); chasing they average 130.3 and win only 28.6%.
  Today they batted first and made 129, nearly 27 runs below their own
  bat-first average and below the TU par band (low 150s over 20 overs,
  from our own 64 matches). One game does not rewrite a 50% record,
  but it does say the batting, not the bowling, lost this one: only
  one Chitwan batter passed 20.
- **The powerplay cost two wickets and never produced a platform.**
  Chitwan lost Dev Khanal at 10/1 in the second over and Arjun Saud at
  27/2 in the fifth, against an S1–S2 powerplay batting average of
  38.1 runs and 2.13 wickets lost per match. The wicket count matches
  their history; the runs behind it did not, because the two men who
  were supposed to build on it both fell below themselves: Dawid Malan
  made 16 off 17 (0.94 runs per ball against a 1.1702 prior) and
  Sikandar Raza, captaining the side, made 15 off 20 (0.75 against a
  1.2941 prior). Arjun Saud's 15 off 16 (0.94 against a 1.1639 prior)
  was below himself too. A powerplay that loses its captain's pace
  and its opener's pace in the same passage leaves one batter to do
  the whole innings.
- **Bopara did the whole innings — and one batter is not a total.**
  Ravi Bopara made 49 off 40 (1.23 runs per ball against a 1.2554
  prior — essentially himself, at his normal pace, not above it),
  with three fours and two sixes, and was Chitwan's only batter past
  20. Bibek Yadav's 17 off 15 was the next best, and Tom Taylor's
  unbeaten 6 off 6 closed an innings that had already stalled: from
  113/5 in the nineteenth over, Chitwan added 16 runs for the last
  wicket. Bopara reaching 10,000 runs in all forms of T20 cricket
  during this innings (Kathmandu Post) is a career milestone, not a
  match-winning one — the innings around him never gave him a partner
  who stayed.
- **The bowling had a cluster and a leak, and the leak mattered more.**
  Ranjit Kumar took 3/28 in 4 overs — Bhandari, McMullen and captain
  Airee — and Mousom Dhakal took 1/19 in 2.4 overs (7.13 an over
  against a 7.04 prior — about himself, and a wicket at the right time).
  But Chitwan conceded nine extras (5 wides, 4 no-balls), and
  Sikandar Raza's spell accounts for six of them: 0/26 in 4 overs
  (6.50 an over against a 7.159 prior — cheaper than himself on runs,
  yet three wides and three no-balls in the delivery counts). A
  defence of 129 cannot give away nine free runs plus six extra
  deliveries and still expect the chase to stall. Tom Taylor, who
  had been Chitwan's not-out finisher with the bat, went 0/24 in
  3 overs with the ball, and Rijan Dhakal's 1/18 in a single over
  was the only other wicket.

## How Sudurpaschim approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A chase win, on schedule — and faster than their history.**
  S1–S2: chasing Sudurpaschim average 134.0 and win 80.0% of those
  games (5 games); batting first they average 156.2 and win 76.9%.
  They won the toss and fielded, and chased 130 in 16.4 overs — fewer
  runs than their chase average, but with 20 balls to spare, which is
  the point: their chase record is built on finishing early, not on
  scraping home. The captain's decision matched the profile, and the
  profile held.
- **The powerplay was lost on wickets and won on intent.**
  Sudurpaschim lost Saif Zaib at 3/1 in the second over and Binod
  Bhandari at 34/2 in the fourth, against an S1–S2 powerplay batting
  average of 40.9 runs and 1.50 wickets lost per match. Two down early
  is worse than their history — but the batter still in at 34/2 was
  McMullen, and he was the innings. Bhandari himself made 11 off 7
  (1.57 against a 1.148 prior) before falling to Ranjit Kumar, and
  Saif Zaib made 2 off 5; neither has a priors row problem to hide

- **The bowling was a squeeze with one exception.** Scott Kuggeleijn

  sides is five runs and five extra deliveries, in a chase won with
  20 balls to spare — not the margin, but the shape of it.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

Janakpur won Match 9 defending 147 — a below-par total defended by a
powerplay squeeze and a captain's control spell. Chitwan tried to
defend 129 tonight, 18 runs fewer, and could not, because their squeeze
never arrived: they took two powerplay wickets and then let McMullen
score at two runs a ball, and their own extras handed the chase nine
runs. The Bolts' blueprint point stands — at TU, a below-par total is
defended by wickets and discipline together, never by one of them.
Sudurpaschim's chase is the other side of the same lesson: a side that
loses its captain first ball and still wins by five wickets with 20
balls to spare is a side whose middle order does not panic, and whose
finishers (Sheikh and Rutherford, both unbeaten) do not need to be
spectacular to be sufficient.
