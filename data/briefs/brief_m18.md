## The one-line read

A match Lumbini lost in the first five overs, not the last five: 28/6
at 4.3 overs chasing 161, and Sundeep Jora's 44 off 45 — 36% of the
total — was the only resistance. Kathmandu's opening stand of 104 was
three times Lumbini's powerplay total of 34, and it was worth more
than the chase ever threatened: from 28/6, Lumbini never got within
60 of the target.

## How Kathmandu approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first total 33 above their average, built on one stand
  and one rescue.** S1–S2 batting first Kathmandu average 126.6 and
  win 44.4% of those games (9 games). Today, put in after losing the
  toss, they made 160/8 — but the shape was two innings in one: a
  104-run opening stand, then a collapse, then a rescue. The
  powerplay was 45/0 against a profile 38.9 runs and 2.53 wickets —
  better on both counts — and the first wicket did not fall until
  13.2 overs.
- **The openers were both on their numbers, and the stand was the
  match.** David Warner made 66 off 50 (1.32 runs per ball against
  a 1.2504 prior) and Sahil Patel 38 off 36 (1.06 against 1.1352)
  in a 104-run stand off 80 balls. Warner's was the only fifty of
  the match and took Player of the Match; Patel's was the quiet
  half of a match-winning partnership, the same role he played in
  the Match 15 win.
- **Then seven wickets for 52 runs — the collapse the profile
  warns about.** From 104/1, Kathmandu lost 7 for 52 to reach
  156/8: Karan KC 9 off 10 (0.90 against a 1.2946 prior), caught
  and bowled by Abhishesh Gautam, William Bosisto 0 off 1 on no
  recorded prior, Rashid Khan 1 off 3 (0.33 against 1.0404),
  Jahandad Khan 6 off 3 (2.00 against 1.2086), and two run outs in
  the death — Mohammad Aadil Alam 1 off 1 and Santosh Yadav 2 off
  1, both run out by Dickwella and Stewart. Bhim Sharki finished 0
  not out off 0. Without Sahan Arachchige's unbeaten 34 off 15
  (2.27 against a 1.5156 death prior), Kathmandu finish on 126 —
  exactly their S1–S2 bat-first average — and Lumbini chase 127,
  not 161.
- **The bowling was three tight spells and two leaks, and the
  tight ones were the whole game.** S1–S2 bowling in the powerplay
  Kathmandu concede 40.1 runs and take 2.06 wickets per match.
  Today they conceded 34 and took 6. Jahandad Khan took 4-0-23-4
  (economy 5.75 against an 8.015 prior, and a 7.571 powerplay
  prior) — all four wickets inside 3.4 overs, four of Lumbini's
  top six. Karan KC took 4-1-24-2 (6.00 against 7.818), including
  the only maiden of the match, in a chase of 161. Shahab Alam
  took 4-0-18-1 (4.50 against 6.513) and Rashid Khan 3-0-20-1
  (6.67 against 7.623). The leaks were Santosh Yadav, 4-0-32-1
  (8.00 against 6.926, the only Kathmandu bowler above his prior),
  and Arachchige's 0.3-0-0-1 that closed the match. Six wickets
  in the powerplay is the profile's best new-ball return of the
  season.

  above it.** S1–S2 chasing Lumbini average 121.7 and win 61.5%

  but the target was 161, and their chase record is built on
  targets near 122, not 161. Their bat-first average of 160.2
  (win rate only 33.3% in 6 games) was irrelevant tonight: they
  won the toss, chose to field, and never got to set the game up.
- **The powerplay was the worst of their season by wickets.**
  S1–S2 batting in the powerplay Lumbini average 43.9 runs and

  D'Arcy Short 4 off 7 (0.57 against a 1.1684 prior), Aasif

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 21, Tue 10 Nov, 17:00 v Sudurpaschim; Match 25, Fri 13 Nov v Chitwan)

1. **The table squeeze is now real, and Tuesday is a six-pointer.**
   Kathmandu's win takes them to 6 points and second place, ahead
   of Janakpur's 4. Sudurpaschim also sit on 6. Janakpur's last two
   league games are both direct clashes: beat Sudurpaschim on
   Tuesday and Janakpur go to 6, level with them; lose, and
   Sudurpaschim go to 8 while Janakpur stay on 4 with only Chitwan
   (5 points) left to play. The M17 brief called Match 25 the
   last-game shootout; Match 21 now decides whether it is played
   for a playoff place or for pride.
2. **The collapse cluster is now five matches running — and the
   killer phase moved earlier.** Match 14: Janakpur 53/3 became
   56/6 in eleven balls. Match 15: Biratnagar 29/3 became 35/7.
   Match 16: Karnali 26/3 inside five overs of a chase. Match 17:
   Pokhara 103/5 became 121 all out. Match 18: Lumbini 28/6
   inside five overs. The M17 brief prescribed a plan for overs
   7–13; M18 adds that the first six overs decide more matches
   than the death does. All six of Lumbini's powerplay wickets
   were new-ball seam — Jahandad four, Karan KC two. For Tuesday,
   that cuts both ways: Janakpur's batting must survive
   Sudurpaschim's new ball, and Janakpur's own new-ball pair —
   Harmeet Singh owns the powerplay in the season blueprint — is
   the weapon that ends games before the middle overs begin.
3. **The one-anchor lesson has now lost three matches in three
   days.** Miller 93 not out (M16), Kumal 45 (M17), Jora 44
   (M18): each was more than a third of his side's total, each
   lost by 30-plus. A batting order needs two contributors, not
   one hero. Janakpur's last two games need a second batter to
   stand with whoever anchors — the blueprint's middle order
   (Neesham at 4–5, Moeen's replacement slot) has to produce, not
   just the opener.
4. **Lumbini are beatable, and the recipe is written down — but
   Janakpur do not face them again in the league.** The team that
   beat them did it with six powerplay wickets and a 104-run
   opening stand of their own. Janakpur's M14 loss to Lumbini was
   the mirror image — a top-order collapse of their own. The
   recipe matters only if the teams meet again in the playoffs,
   and the same applies to Kathmandu: Warner anchors on his prior
   (66 off 50) and Arachchige finishes above his (34 not out off
   15). Any playoff death plan has to account for Arachchige, not
   just Warner.
5. **The lock's favourite lost again, and the toss did not decide
   it.** Lumbini won the toss, fielded, and the model made them
   favourites at 0.5263 — then they were bowled out for 124.
   Chasing at Kirtipur under lights is not the edge the toss
   suggested today, the second match running. For Tuesday's toss
   call, that is two data points, not a rule — but it is two data
   points in the same direction.
