# Match 31 post-match brief — what Sudurpaschim v Chitwan tells the Janakpur Bolts

**Result:** Sudurpaschim Royals won by 3 wickets. Chitwan Rhinos 133
for 9 in 20 overs; Sudurpaschim Royals 136 for 7 in 19.5 overs,
chasing 134, with one ball to spare. Chitwan are eliminated.
Sudurpaschim meet Lumbini Lions in Saturday's Final.

## How Chitwan approached it

Won the toss and chose to bat — the minority choice for them: they
have fielded first in 60% of the tosses they have won. Their bat-first
average across the profile's ten bat-first matches is 153.3; tonight
they made 133, twenty below it, and it was never enough on a night
their bowling could not defend it either.

The start went against them at once. Kamil Mishara, a day after his
record 120 not out against Kathmandu, edged Scott Kuggeleijn to
Bhandari for 4 off 3, and Dev Khanal followed for 6 off 9; Chitwan
were 15 for 2 inside 2.3 overs. Ravi Bopara made 1 off 8 and they
were 31 for 3 at 5.4. The middle order then stalled completely:
Bibek Kumar Yadav 3 off 11, Kushal Malla 1 off 5, Usama Mir 2 off 5,
and Chitwan slid from 58 for 4 at 9.5 to 62 for 6 at 11.4.

Dawid Malan held one end through all of it. His 71 not out off 51
(nine fours, two sixes) came at 1.39 runs per ball against a prior of
1.1702 — above his number, and his second half-century of the
tournament. The only support came late: Salahuddin Khan 18 off 16 and
Kamal Singh Airee 10 off 8, who added 40 and 14 with Malan for the
seventh and eighth wickets. Rijan Dhakal was run out for 3 off the
final ball. The death produced 31 runs from 102 for 7 at 16.5,
about on Chitwan's death-bat profile average of 33.4 per match. The
innings also leaked 14 extras, 11 of them wides — ten from Hemant
Dhami alone.

## How Sudurpaschim approached it

A chase, which is their stronger suit: eight chases in the profile at
an average of 127.4 and a 75% win rate, against a target of 134 —
just above that average. They made it, but only just, and the shape
of the chase was a collapse followed by one man holding it together.

The powerplay produced 41 runs, on their profile average of 43.7, but
cost two wickets (Munsey 23-1 at 3.6, Bhandari 31-2 at 4.4) against a
profile 1.73. Then it got worse: Ishan Pandey fell at 43 for 3 in
the 6.4th over and Scott Kuggeleijn — surprisingly sent in at number
three, per the Kathmandu Post — made 2 off 5, leaving them 45 for 4
at 7.2.

Tharindu Ratnayake rebuilt it in three stands. With captain Dipendra
Singh Airee (15 off 16) he added 31 to take them to 76 for 5 at 12.3;
with Aarif Sheikh (10 off 10) he added 26 to 102 for 6 at 15.5; with
Fabian Allen (run out 5 off 10) he added 30 to 132 for 7 at 19.4.
With two needed from the final two deliveries, Ratnayake struck a
boundary — 53 not out off 42, seven fours, at 1.26 runs per ball
against a prior of 1.2156, on his number with the bat, and Dipak
Bohara 0 not out at the other end.

The bowling had set the chase up. Kuggeleijn took 3 for 27 in four
overs (6.75 an over against a prior economy of 6.84 — on his number),
Ratnayake 2 for 23 (5.75 against 7.026 — better), Airee 4-1-20-1
(5.00 against 6.998 — better, including a maiden), and Pandey
3-0-17-1 (5.67 against 7.23 — better). Dhami's 4-0-32-1 (8.00 against
7.5 — slightly worse) carried the ten wides, and Anil Kharel's single
over went for 11 (11.00 against 7.402).

## Players against their priors

Malan's 71 off 51 is the innings of the match in a losing cause: 1.39
against 1.1702. Everything else in Chitwan's top order went the other
way. Mishara's 4 off 3 the day after 120 not out is the comedown
against a prior of 1.3184; Bopara's 1 off 8 came at 0.13 against
1.2554, far below. Salahuddin's 18 off 16 came at 1.13 against 1.2154,
about on his number, while his bowling — 3-0-30-1 at 10.00 against a
prior economy of 7.04 — was the most expensive Chitwan spell.
Kamal Singh Airee's 10 off 8 came at 1.25 against 1.1658, about on,
and his 4-0-26-1 at 6.50 against 8.026 was better than his prior.
Rijan Dhakal's 4-0-26-1 at 6.50 against 7.478 was better too, and his
wicket of Munsey was his 17th of the tournament, keeping him top of
the bowling charts. Usama Mir's 4-0-14-1 at 3.50 against 6.861 was
the most economical spell of the match — and still on the losing
side. Ranjit Kumar's 3-0-25-2 made him Chitwan's joint-highest
wicket-taker; he has no row in the priors file, so no comparison is
recorded.

For Sudurpaschim, the misses were at the top. George Munsey's 8 off
15 came at 0.53 against a prior of 1.4104, far below — the Kathmandu
Post notes he "once again failed to make an impact". Binod Bhandari's
23 off 13 came at 1.77 against 1.148, above his number. Airee's 15
off 16 with the bat came at 0.94 against 1.382, below, and Fabian
Allen's 5 off 10 came at 0.50 against 1.2027, below. Ishan Pandey's
10 off 9 came at 1.11 against 1.1094, exactly on his number, and
Aarif Sheikh's 10 off 10 came at 1.00 against 1.1919, below.

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts

Nothing in the fixture sense: the Bolts were eliminated on 13 Nov,
when this same Chitwan side beat them in the must-win Match 25, and
they have no matches left. The Final, on Saturday 21 Nov at 17:00 at
Kirtipur, is Sudurpaschim Royals against Lumbini Lions — a rematch of
Qualifier 1, which Lumbini won by six wickets on Tuesday, and of
last season's final, which Lumbini also won. Sudurpaschim are in
their third straight final and bidding for a first title: they lost
the inaugural final to Janakpur Bolts and last year's to Lumbini.
For the project, the season ends with that Final (Match 32); this
results job runs once more after it, and the morning cricsheet
ingest will reconcile the provisional record in due course.
