## The one-line read

Two nights after being bowled out for 87, the league's benchmark side produced the season's most controlled chase: they lost three wickets by the seventh over and still cruised, because Dipendra Singh Airee anchored exactly the phase where Sudurpaschim are supposed to be vulnerable. Pokhara's 145 was a stall, not a platform: their middle order scored at under a run a ball for seven overs, and only Arjun Gharti's finish made it look respectable.

## How Sudurpaschim approach games (S1–S2 evidence, updated tonight)

- **The Match 1 collapse was the exception; this is the rule.** S1–S2: 14 wins from 18, and a chase record that is elite in a small sample: 4 wins from 5 chases. Tonight they chased 146 in 16.5 overs at 8.79 an over, their fastest template yet. Do not reprice them off one bad night.
- **They survived the one phase that is supposed to beat them.** The standing read (Match 1 brief) was that early wickets are the only way in: Bhandari fell at 0.5 overs, McMullen at 4.4, Zaib at 6.3, and they were 56/3. Then Airee (51 not out off 36, 1.42 per ball against a 1.382 prior: himself, on schedule) and Ishan Pandey (37 off 31, 1.19 against a 1.109 prior) added 74 for the fourth wicket and the game ended as a contest. Early wickets are necessary against them. Tonight proves they are no longer sufficient on their own; the plan also has to separate Airee from the chase.
- **Their bowling strangle is by committee.** Anil Kharel took the spine of the innings (Mahedi, Shakib, Samarawickrama) with 3/23; Hemant Dhami went 3-0-14-1 (4.67 an over against a 7.50 prior); Airee bowled 4-0-21-1 (5.25 against 6.998). Their S1–S2 powerplay bowling base (2.56 wickets per match, the league's best) showed up on schedule too: two wickets inside the first six overs.
- **The one release valve is Kuggeleijn.** He went 4-0-49-1, 12.25 an over against a 6.84 prior, nearly double his rate. Every other Sudurpaschim bowler went at 8.00 or less. Against this attack, the scoring has to be aimed, not spread.

## How Pokhara approach games (S1–S2 evidence, confirmed tonight)

- **A below-average side that bats to its average.** S1–S2: 4 wins from 14, batting first average 147.5 with a 33% win rate, chasing worse (144.0, 25%). Tonight: 145/7 batting first. They are what the profile says.
- **Their innings stalls in the middle.** The powerplay was fine on runs (49/2, against a 45.6-runs base) but overs 6 to 13 produced 34 runs and three wickets: Shakib 14 off 16 (0.88 per ball against a 1.163 prior), Samarawickrama 8 off 9, Lakshan 11 off 16 (0.69). Bhurtel, their highest-prior batter, made 21 off 17 (1.24 against 1.461): below himself again.
- **Two genuine assets.** Mahedi Hasan is in form in both disciplines: 32 off 21 opening (1.52 per ball against a 1.222 prior, and a 1.016 powerplay prior) plus 4-0-31-1 with the ball. And Arjun Gharti at seven is a real finisher: 40 not out off 25 (1.60 per ball) dragged 117/7 to 145. Pokhara are never dead while he is in; plan for him explicitly.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **The season's pattern is now three matches deep.** Match 1 was decided by powerplay wickets, Match 2 by a death collapse, Match 4 by a middle-overs stall and an anchor who refused to move. The common thread for the Bolts: innings at TU are lost in one bad phase, and Janakpur's two weakest phases on the S1–S2 evidence (powerplay batting 2.06 wickets lost per match, death batting 25.2 runs per match) are exactly the phases that keep deciding these games. There is no new lesson here, just a third confirmation.
- **Par discipline still holds.** 145 was below par and was chased with 19 balls to spare; 157 was defended in Match 2. The low-150s TU par from the pre-season tables stands, and the difference between the two nights was wickets: Chitwan took 10, Pokhara took 4.

## What this changes for the Bolts

**Match 9 v Pokhara (Sun 1 Nov, 17:00):**
1. Squeeze the middle overs. Pokhara's overseas spine (Shakib, Samarawickrama) is crawling between overs 6 and 13; that is precisely where Janakpur's spin trio is built to bowl. Win that phase and Pokhara are a 140 side.
2. Do not relax at seven down. Gharti's 40 not out off 25 turned a 125-type score into 145. The death plan (Moeen first, then Iftikhar/Aagri) has to be bowled to him as a set finisher, not as a tailender.
3. Mahedi Hasan is the dual threat: attack his bowling early rather than letting him settle (he took Pandey tonight and went at 7.75), and do not let him open freely with the bat.

**Match 21 v Sudurpaschim (Tue 10 Nov, 17:00):**

Early wickets remain the entry point (they were 56/3 tonight), but the game plan now needs a specific Airee plan on top: he is their chase engine (51 not out tonight; death prior 1.741, the highest on their card) and the field and matchups must be set to make someone else finish the game.
2. Aim the batting at Kuggeleijn. On tonight's evidence he is the one Sudurpaschim bowler a side can go at (12.25 an over); Kharel, Dhami and Airee combined went at under 6. Manufacture the innings so the set batter faces Kuggeleijn's overs, not theirs.
