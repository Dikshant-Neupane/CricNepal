## The one-line read

A below-par total defended by a powerplay squeeze and a captain's control spell,
not by late hitting alone. Janakpur made 147 — just below the TU par band (low
150s over 20 overs, from our own 64 matches) — and then took two wickets inside
the powerplay, removed Pokhara's captain first ball of the seventh over, and
collapsed their middle order from 53/3 to 87/7. Pokhara's late pair added 22
runs for the eighth wicket and still finished 9 short, because the game had
already been lost between overs 7 and 13. The two-way performances decided it:
Moeen Ali (21 off 16, then 3/13 in 4 overs) and Shakib Al Hasan (3/15 in 4 overs
with the ball, 9 off 17 with the bat) — one of them won his team the game, the
other could not do both jobs in the same night.

## How Janakpur approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first win against their own history.** S1–S2: batting first Janakpur
  average 136.4 and win only 20.0% of those games (5 games); chasing they average
  132.1 and win 58.3%. Today they batted first, made 147 — above their S1–S2
  bat-first average but below par — and won. One game does not rewrite a 20%
  record, and the win came from the defence, not the total: the batting itself
  was ordinary, the bowling won it.
- **The powerplay was quiet, not explosive — and that was enough.** Janakpur
  were 47/1 at 6.3 overs, effectively one down through the powerplay, against an
  S1–S2 powerplay batting average of 42.8 runs and 2.06 wickets lost per match.
  Moeen Ali (21 off 16, 1.31 runs per ball against a 1.1443 prior) gave the
  innings its only early pace; Trit Raj Das anchored from the other end.
  Pokhara's bowling was respectable without being threatening — Mahedi Hasan's
  1/24 in 4 overs included a maiden — but nobody on either side won the
  powerplay outright, which suited the side defending 147.
- **Trit Raj Das was exactly himself, and that is the point.** 61 off 55 (1.11
  runs per ball against a 1.1159 prior), seven fours and two sixes, out at 117
  in the 18th over. He neither accelerated nor stalled relative to his own
  record: he held one end for 55 balls while partners came and went (Saif Hassan
  1 off 9, Harmeet Singh 9 off 8, Anil Sah 9 off 9 — all below their priors),
  and his wicket at 17.1 left the finish to others. An anchor innings is only
  worth what the finish adds to it, and tonight the finish added plenty.
- **Abhisekh Tiwari finished what Trit started.** 24 not out off 10 (2.40 runs
  per ball against a 1.1263 prior; his death prior is 1.417), one four and two
  sixes, arriving after Trit's dismissal with the score 117/5 and pushing it to
  147. Without those 24, Janakpur defend 123 and lose; with them, 147 proved
  exactly enough. The death-batting role the blueprint worries about had an
  answer tonight, from a domestic batter rather than an overseas finisher.
- **James Neesham returned — and was quiet.** Benched for Match 5 for personal
  reasons (per the Match 9 card), he returned tonight and made 11 off 11 (1.00
  against a 1.302 prior) and took 1/16 in 2 overs (8.00 an over against a 7.958
  prior — exactly himself with the ball). No conclusion is drawn from one
  quiet return game; the note for Tuesday is simply that he is back in the XI.
- **The XI note, including the known gap.** Janakpur's XI was Moeen Ali,
  Trit Raj Das, Saif Hassan, Harmeet Singh (captain), Anil Sah (wicketkeeper),
  James Neesham, Abhisekh Tiwari, Amar Routela, Lalit Rajbanshi, Mayan Yadav
  and Rahul Yadav. Lalit, Mayan and Rahul did not bat. Rahul Yadav bowled
  tonight (0/23 in 2 overs, including a no-ball) and is still absent from
  player_master.csv — the same registry blocker that cost the Match 9 P1 lock
  — so Tuesday's XI validation will fail the same way if he is selected again.
  Parvez Hossain Emon, signed 29 Oct, did not play again.

## How Pokhara approach games — and what changed tonight

- **A chase side that cannot chase yet.** S1–S2: Pokhara chase at 144.0 on
  average and win only 25.0% of their chases (8 games); batting first they
  average 147.5 and win 33.3%. Tonight they chased 148 and made 138/9. The
  pattern held: their chase batting is not built to overhaul a par-plus total
  once the top order goes, and tonight the top order went early.
- **Kushal Bhurtel was exactly himself — and alone.** 35 off 24 (1.46 runs per
  ball against a 1.4606 prior), five fours, the highest-prior batter on either
  side doing precisely what his record says. He was the third wicket, first ball
  of the seventh over (53/3 at 6.3), and nobody replaced him: the next four
  wickets added 34 runs in seven overs, and by 87/7 in the 14th over the chase
  needed a miracle rather than a finish. The Match 9 card called Bhurtel's
  wicket the one that matters most; tonight it mattered exactly that much.
- **Shakib Al Hasan won the bowling and lost the batting.** With the ball he
  was the match's best bowler: 3/15 in 4 overs (3.75 an over against a 6.754
  prior), removing Moeen, Saif Hassan and Harmeet — three of Janakpur's top
  four. With the bat he made 9 off 17 (0.53 against a 1.1634 prior), caught off
  Saif Hassan at 83/5. A side whose best bowler is also its most important
  middle-order batter cannot afford him failing in one discipline; tonight he
  failed in the one his team needed second.
- **The finisher warning did not materialise.** The Match 9 card warned that
  Pokhara are never dead while Arjun Gharti is in (40 not out off 25 in Match
  4). Tonight Gharti made 7 off 15 (0.47 against a 1.1128 prior), caught off
  Saif Hassan at 72/4, and the late hitting came instead from the tail:
  Bipin Acharya 20 off 19 (1.05 against a 1.1731 prior) and Bipin Khatri 17 not
  out off 14 (1.21 against a 1.1471 prior), who added 22 for the eighth wicket
  before Acharya fell at 132/9 in the 20th over. The last 10 balls, with 20
  needed, produced 10 runs. Gharti failing early is now a data point, not just
  a possibility, for any future Pokhara card.
- **The selection question from the card resolved both ways.** The card asked
  whether Mohammad Saifuddin or Chamika Karunaratne would come in; both did.
  Saifuddin took Anil Sah's wicket but went for 17 in his single over (17.00
  against a 7.587 prior) and made 0 off 2 with the bat; Karunaratne took 2/23 in
  3 overs (7.67 against a 7.89 prior — at himself) and made 4 off 11. In came
  Bipin Acharya too; out went Sadeera Samarawickrama, Dhananjaya Lakshan and
  Aakash Chand from the card's likely XI. Mahedi Hasan opened again and made 8
  off 4 before Moeen removed him; Dinesh Kharel made 3 off 9, stumped off Moeen.
- **Their bowling had one elite spell and no support for it.** After Shakib's
  3/15, the next-best Pokhara figures were Mahedi's 1/24 in 4 overs (6.00
  against a 7.228 prior) and Kushal Bhurtel's 0/24 in 4 overs (6.00 against a
  7.569 prior). Chamika Karunaratne was at himself, Pratik Pokhrel (0/21 in 2)
  and Bipin Khatri (0/18 in 2) went wicketless, and Saifuddin's one over cost
  17. Shakib cannot defend 147 alone; tonight nobody else took more than one
  wicket.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **The blueprint's powerplay bowling plan worked — with a correction.** The
  blueprint gives Harmeet Singh the powerplay overs (economy 5.89 in the
  blueprint's numbers; 6.634 prior overall). Tonight the powerplay wickets came
  from Moeen Ali, not Harmeet: Mahedi at 1.1 overs and Kharel at 3.6, both in
  the powerplay, in a 4-1-13-3 spell (3.25 an over against a 6.815 prior).
  Harmeet then took 2/20 in 4 overs (5.00 against a 6.634 prior) through the
  middle, including the caught-and-bowled of Karunaratne. The lesson is not
  "Harmeet owns the powerplay" but "Moeen's early overs work": Moeen removed
  both openers inside the first four overs, and Harmeet's control
  through the middle finished the squeeze. Tuesday's card should plan Moeen
  for early overs again, not just Harmeet.
- **Saif Hassan is expensive and wicket-taking — both, at once.** 2/36 in 4
  overs (9.00 an over against a 7.142 prior), but his two wickets were Shakib
  and Gharti, Pokhara's middle-order core, both inside the collapse from 53/3
  to 87/7. Expensive wicket-takers are worth carrying when the wickets are the
  right ones; the risk is the over that goes for 17-plus, which tonight was
  Saifuddin's, not Saif's. The Tuesday card should price Saif as a
  wicket-taker who leaks, not as a control bowler.
- **Lalit Rajbanshi did his job quietly.** 1/26 in 4 overs (6.50 against a
  6.789 prior), and the wicket was Bhurtel. Against a side whose captain is
  its highest-prior batter, a control spell plus that wicket is a full
  contribution, even without a headline figure.
- **One bat-first win does not fix the bat-first record.** S1–S2 says Janakpur
  win 20.0% batting first; tonight makes it one S3 bat-first win from one S3
  bat-first game. The batting shape tonight — anchor plus late domestic hitting,
  middle order below par — is not a template to repeat deliberately; it is a
  total that survived because the bowling defended it. If Tuesday's toss goes
  the same way, the plan must still be 160-plus, not 147-and-hope.
