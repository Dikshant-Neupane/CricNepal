## The one-line read

Janakpur's first win of Season 3 came the way the blueprint says Bolts games are won: wickets through the innings, then a chase that never needed a finish. Kathmandu were bowled out for a total 35 runs below the TU par in the low 150s, and Janakpur lost only two wickets chasing it — but the win also broke the plan in one place that matters: Moeen Ali opened the batting, the one role the card explicitly forbade, and made 58 not out. A plan deviation that wins by eight wickets has to be examined, not celebrated or dismissed.

## How Kathmandu approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first side that batted first and still fell short of itself.** S1–S2: batting first they average 128.0 and win 50.0% of those games. Today they made 115 — below that average, below the Day-slot first-innings mean of 146.8, and their profile already records 6 sub-120 totals in 15 S1–S2 matches — today adds another. This was not a par score defended badly; it was a below-par score, and it lost.
- **Their known powerplay leak opened the game.** S1–S2 Kathmandu lose 2.53 powerplay wickets per match with the bat, the worst powerplay batting number in the profile. Today they lost three inside the powerplay: Heazlett at 6/1 (1.1 overs), Harishankar Shah at 19/2 (2.6), Smit Patel at 29/3 (5.1). The fall-of-wickets line is the profile, played out in order.
- **Only two batters were themselves.** Bhim Sharki top-scored with 33 off 29 (1.14 runs per ball against a 1.1926 prior — himself, near enough) and Rashid Khan made 17 off 14 (1.21 against 1.1761 — himself). Nobody else passed 13. Sam Heazlett, the one card-named threat who actually played, made 1 off 3 (0.33 against a 1.2730 prior); captain Karan KC made 8 off 18 (0.44 against a 1.2946 prior). Those two failures are the innings: the overseas opener and the captain combined for 9 runs off 21 balls.
- **The middle held, then the innings collapsed in a cluster.** From 47/4 (7.4 overs) Kathmandu reached 92/6 when Bhim Sharki was stumped at 14.3 — a recovery of sorts, built on Bhim and Sumit Maharjan (10 off 9). Then three wickets fell for 7 runs: Karan KC at 99/7 (16.5), Mohammad Aadil Alam first ball at 99/8 (16.6), Jahandad Khan at 99/9 (17.1). Only Ripon Mondol (9 off 8) and Shahab Alam (4 not out) pushed the total from 99 to 115. Wickets in clusters, again, decided a TU game — the same shape as Matches 2, 3 and 4.
- **Their bowling took only one bowler's wicket and never threatened the chase.** Ripon Mondol's 3-0-17-1 was the only bowler's wicket; the second wicket to fall was a run out. Shahab Alam, their best bowler on priors (economy 6.513), went 3-0-18-0 (6.00 an over — better than his prior, but wicketless); Nepal's Rashid Khan went 1-0-5-0 (5.00 against a 6.629 prior); Karan KC went 2-0-18-0 (9.00 against a 7.818 prior — the one bowler Janakpur could go at, and the chase never needed to). Nine bowlers were used and none took a second wicket, because the chase was 116, not 150.

## How Janakpur approach games — and where today broke the plan

- **Five bowlers, and every one of them did the job.** Janakpur used only five bowlers, and all five took a wicket. Rahul Yadav took 4/31 in four overs and was Player of the Match — his second strong S3 outing after 2/15 against Karnali in Match 3. Saif Hassan took 2/11 in four overs (2.75 an over), the tightest spell of the match. Moeen Ali took 1/23 (5.75 against a 6.815 prior — better than his base), Harmeet Singh 1/18 in 3.3 overs (5.14 against a 6.634 prior, and better than his 5.887 powerplay economy prior), and Lalit Rajbanshi 1/30. No bowler went at more than 7.75, and the innings closed at 19.3 overs.
- **The powerplay bowling finally matches the profile's other half.** S1–S2 Janakpur take only 1.00 powerplay wicket per match with the ball. Today they took three in the powerplay, for the second match running after Match 3 — Rahul Yadav took two of them (both openers) and Saif Hassan the third. Two matches is not a new profile, but the old "never takes powerplay wickets" line is now two games out of date.
- **Moeen opened — and the forbidden role produced the match-winning innings.** The Match 5 card was explicit: "Moeen Ali — never before No. 5. His powerplay runs per ball (0.907) is the side's weakest; his value is the middle (1.0668) and the death (1.5529)." Today he opened, at No. 2, and made 58 not out off 51 balls (1.14 per ball, exactly his 1.1443 overall prior), with three fours and three sixes. He did not stall in the powerplay this time: he was 18 off 15 at the five-over mark. The honest read is narrow — opening worked against a 116 chase and an attack that took only one bowler's wicket, not against a par total or a wicket-taking attack. It does not rewrite the blueprint, which revises only at the match-10 and match-20 checkpoints, but it does mean the "never opens" line now carries a counterexample that must be weighed at match 10, not ignored.
- **The chase passed every benchmark Match 3 failed.** The card's checks were: at most 2 wickets down after 6 overs, and one top-five batter still batting at over 15. Janakpur were 37/2 at the fall of the second wicket (5.1 overs) and finished with both set batters unbeaten at 17.2. Saif Hassan made 40 not out off 39 (1.03 per ball) in an unbroken 79-run third-wicket stand with Moeen — the partnership that ended the game as a contest. Trit Raj Das made 5 off 5 (1.00 against a 1.1159 prior — near himself) and Mayan Yadav 9 off 10 (0.90 against a 1.078 prior), run out for the second match running after his Match 3 run out. The run-out habit, not the top order's rate, is the one batting flaw that survived the win.
- **The XI itself is the availability news.** Janakpur's XI on the night was Trit Raj Das, Moeen Ali, Mayan Yadav, Saif Hassan, Kusal Perera, Anil Sah, Lalit Rajbanshi, Abhisekh Tiwari, Amar Routela, Rahul Yadav and Harmeet Singh (captain). Jimmy Neesham did not play, and neither did Iftikhar Ahmed or Hunain Shah — the PCB NOC doubt that kept them out of Match 3 still has no resolution in any source read for this match. On Kathmandu's side, none of the card's expected overseas core played: no David Warner, no K. S. Bharat, no Will Bosisto, no Sahan Arachchige. Their overseas presence on the night was Sam Heazlett and Smit Patel. Smit Patel is still not in the verified project squad data (single-source at the T-48h sweep); his appearance on this scorecard is the second source the registry needs, flagged for the next squad integration, not assumed.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **115 is now the third below-par total in five matches, and all three lost.** Chitwan defended 157, Karnali defended 132, and Kathmandu could not defend 115 — the difference, every time, was wickets: the defending side took 10, 10 and 2 wickets respectively in those three games. Janakpur took 10 today. The TU par tables describe the average game; wickets through the middle overs decide the actual one.
- **The chase template is now proven at low totals and unproven at par.** Janakpur have chased 133 and failed (Match 3, all out 113) and chased 116 and won with eight wickets in hand. The gap between those two chases is 17 runs and eight wickets. Sunday's game against Pokhara, a side that made 145 batting first in Match 4, will test the middle of that range.
- **Rahul Yadav is now a form line, not a one-match story.** Two S3 outings: 2/15 against Karnali, 4/31 and Player of the Match against Kathmandu. No priors row exists for him yet, so there is no versus-prior number to quote — but the wickets are in the capture, and the next opponent card should price him as a wicket-taker, not a debutant filler.
