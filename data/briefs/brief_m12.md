**Result:** Biratnagar Kings won by 2 runs. Biratnagar Kings 174/3 in

## The one-line read

A chase that was rescued twice and then lost in the final over.
Janakpur were 30/3 in the fifth over, were carried back into the game
by a 76-run fourth-wicket stand and then a 56-run fifth-wicket stand,
and still needed 13 off the last over — and Andre Russell, who had
already made an unbeaten 52, took two wickets in that over and defended
it. Biratnagar's win was built the other way round: three fifties, only
three wickets lost, and one man doing the damage at both ends. Two runs
is the margin, but the shapes of the two innings were not close:
Biratnagar never lost control of their innings; Janakpur spent theirs
catching up.

## How Biratnagar approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first total well above their own history — and above par.**
  S1–S2: batting first Biratnagar average 151.9 and win 42.9% of those
  games (7 games). Today, sent in after losing the toss, they made
  174/3 — more than 22 runs above their own bat-first average and well
  above the TU par band (low 150s over 20 overs, from our own 64
  matches). A bat-first side that loses only three wickets while
  clearing par by that much has done the hard part before the chase
  starts.
- **The powerplay was steady, not explosive — and that was enough.**
  S1–S2: Biratnagar's powerplay batting averages 42.6 runs and 2.06
  wickets lost per match. Today the mandatory powerplay produced 46
  runs for one wicket: Shehan Jayasuriya fell for 1 off 3 in the first
  over, stumped off Moeen Ali, and after that Lokesh Bam and Imran
  Sheikh were never separated until the fourteenth over. The
  second-wicket stand was worth 107 runs in 76 balls (2/1 to 109/2),
  which is the innings: two men set the platform, and Russell finished
  it.
- **Three fifties, each at or above the man — except the keeper.**
  Lokesh Bam made 50 off 40 (1.25 runs per ball against a 1.34 prior —
  slightly below himself, but the innings' anchor: he was still there
  at 146/3 in the eighteenth over). Imran Sheikh made 56 off 45
  (1.24 against a 1.1555 prior — above himself). Andre Russell made
  52 not out off 29 (1.79 against a 1.3321 prior — far above himself),
  with two fours and five sixes, and Shubham Ranjane finished 7 not
  out off 5 (1.40 against a 1.1784 prior). Only one Biratnagar batter
  failed, and he is the one the project still cannot price:
  Jayasuriya has no priors row and no master entry (the same gap that
  blocked this match's post-XI lock), so there is no versus-prior read
  for his 1 off 3 — only the note that the 39 off 11 he made in
  Match 8 did not repeat.
- **The bowling was expensive everywhere except where it had to be
  cheap — and Russell's final over covered the rest.** Biratnagar used
  seven bowlers. Angelo Mathews took 2/25 in 3 overs (8.33 an over
  against a 7.126 prior — more expensive than himself, but both
  Janakpur openers' stand broken: Trit Raj Das and then Bishal
  Shusling). Sandeep Lamichhane went 0/27 in 4 overs (6.75 against a
  6.461 prior — about himself, no wicket). Aprajit Poudel went 0/26 in
  4 overs (6.50 against a 7.04 debutant default — cheaper than the
  default, but the default is not a measured history). Basir Ahamad
  went 0/16 in 2 overs (8.00 against a 7.087 prior) but took three
  catches — Saif Hassan, Bishal Shusling and Harmeet Singh, all off
  Russell or Mathews. Rupesh Singh went 1/21 in 2 overs (10.50 an
  over) for the wicket of James Neesham, and Shehan Jayasuriya bowled
  a single over for 6. Russell himself went 4/45 in 4 overs (11.25 an
  over against a 7.892 prior — far more expensive than himself), yet
  he took Moeen Ali, Anil Sah, Harmeet Singh and Saif Hassan, the
  last two in the final over. Expensive figures with four wickets,
  including the two that decided the game, are not a bad spell; they
  are a match-winning one.

## How Janakpur approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A chase win turned into a chase loss by two runs — on a total
  above their chase history.** S1–S2: chasing Janakpur average 132.1
  and win 58.3% of those games (12 games). Today they chased 175, made
  172, and lost. The target was about 43 runs above their chase
  average, so this is not a below-par chase that fell short; it is a
  big chase that fell two runs short, and the reason sits in the first
  six overs, not the last one.
- **The powerplay lost three wickets and never gave the chase a
  platform.** S1–S2: Janakpur's powerplay batting averages 42.8 runs
  and 2.06 wickets lost per match. Today the powerplay produced 40
  runs for three wickets: Trit Raj Das bowled by Mathews for 6 off 9
  (0.67 runs per ball against a 1.1159 prior — well below himself),
  Moeen Ali caught for 10 off 14 (0.71 against a 1.1443 prior — well
  below himself), and Anil Sah caught for 0 off 2 in the same over,
  at 30/3. Two notes on that. First, the blueprint's opener did not
  open: Anil Sah, whom the blueprint has opening, batted at four and
  made a duck, while Moeen and Trit opened. Second, the rescue that
  followed was real but it was always a rescue: Saif Hassan and
  Bishal Shusling added 76 in 56 balls for the fourth wicket
  (30/3 to 106/4), and Saif and James Neesham then added 56 in 30
  balls (106/4 to 162/5).
- **Saif Hassan was above himself, and the innings still needed him
  to be more.** Saif made 71 off 48 (1.48 runs per ball against a
  1.1949 prior — well above himself), with two fours and five sixes,
  after 40 not out off 39 in Match 5 and 1 off 9 in Match 9. This was
  the rebound the chase was built on, and it ended on the last ball,
  caught off Russell at 172/7. Bishal Shusling — listed in the
  project priors as Bishal Susling, with a debutant row and no
  measured history — made 43 off 30 (1.43 against the 1.1731 debutant
  default, which is not a measured comparison). James Neesham made 27
  off 15 (1.80 against a 1.302 prior — well above himself), with three
  sixes, before falling to Rupesh Singh at 162/5 in the nineteenth
  over. Harmeet Singh, the captain, made 0 off 1 (against a 1.218
  prior) in the final over, and Abhisekh Tiwari finished 5 not out
  off 2.
- **The bowling took the wickets that mattered and still went for
  174.** Janakpur used six bowlers. Abhisekh Tiwari took 2/38 in 4
  overs (9.50 an over against a 7.04 prior — more expensive than
  himself, but both wickets were fifty-makers, Bam and Imran Sheikh).
  Moeen Ali took 1/13 in 2 overs (6.50 against a 6.815 prior — slightly
  cheaper than himself, and the first-over strike that removed
  Jayasuriya). Harmeet Singh went 0/35 in 4 overs (8.75 against a
  6.634 prior — more expensive than himself, no wicket). Rahul Yadav
  went 0/29 in 4 overs (7.25 against a 7.04 debutant default — about
  the default, no wicket). Lalit Rajbanshi went 0/30 in 4 overs (no
  priors row, so no versus-prior read). James Neesham went 0/28 in 2
  overs (14.00 against a 7.958 prior — far more expensive than
  himself, no wicket). The discipline problem was concentrated, not
  general: Biratnagar's innings carried 8 extras (5 wides, 2
  no-balls), and Tiwari's spell alone accounts for 2 wides and both
  no-balls. Janakpur's own innings carried 10 extras (4 byes, 2
  leg-byes, 3 wides, 1 no-ball).
- **The toss decision matched the profile; the powerplay did not
  repay it.** S1–S2: Janakpur field first in 66.7% of the tosses they
  win (9 tosses). They won this toss and fielded, as the profile
  would have them do. With the ball, their powerplay conceded 46
  runs for one wicket, against an S1–S2 powerplay bowling average of
  40.2 conceded and 1.0 wicket taken per match — more runs than
  history, exactly the history wicket count. Biratnagar's powerplay
  bowling, by contrast, conceded 40 for three wickets, against their
  S1–S2 average of 45.1 conceded and 1.75 taken: tighter than their
  history and wicket-heavier. The game was, in large part, the
  difference between those two powerplays.

## What this means for Janakpur's upcoming fixtures

- **Next up is Lumbini on Thu 5 Nov at 12:30 (Match 14, TU
  Kirtipur)** — the opponent card for that fixture is already filed.
  The Bolts go into it 2–2, not 3–1, and the gap between those two
  records is a top order that lost three wickets for 30. The fix the
  blueprint already names is the opener: Anil Sah opening, not
  batting at four. Today he did not open and made a duck at four;
  whether that was a one-match reshuffle or a change of plan is the
  first selection question for Thursday, and it is a question, not a
  conclusion — one game does not rewrite the blueprint.
- **Moeen Ali's stint is the availability question for Thursday.**
  The Match 12 opponent card records Moeen on a four-match stint
  (his own words, Kathmandu Post, 29 Oct): Matches 3, 5 and 9 played
  before today, so today was the fourth. Unless an extension is
  reported, Thursday may be played without the man who struck in the
  first over today, made 10 with the bat, and has been the Bolts'
  early-overs bowler in this run. Do not assume he is gone — assume
  the stint question has to be answered before the XI is named, and
  that his overs, if he is absent, have to come from somewhere other
  than an already expensive Neesham spell (0/28 in 2 overs today).
- **The middle order is not the problem; the start is.** Saif's 71,
  Bishal's 43 and Neesham's 27 are all above their priors or, in
  Bishal's case, above the debutant default. A side whose 4–6 can add
  76 and then 56 does not need a new middle order. It needs its first
  six overs to lose one wicket instead of three, so that the same
  middle order is consolidating a chase instead of rescuing one.
  Against Lumbini, whose S1–S2 powerplay bowling takes 2.0 wickets
  per match, that first-six-overs discipline is the game.
- **The final-over lesson cuts both ways.** Russell defended 13 with
  two wickets; Janakpur's chase needed 13 and scored 10, losing two
  wickets in the over. The Bolts' own death bowling was not tested
  in the same way today — Biratnagar lost only three wickets all
  innings — so there is no new death-bowling evidence here, good or
  bad. What there is, is a reminder that a chase that needs 13 off
  the last over against a bowler with four wickets already is a chase
  that has left itself too much to do, however well the middle order
  batted.
