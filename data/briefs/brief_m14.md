# Match 14 post-match brief — what Lumbini v Janakpur tells the Janakpur Bolts

Prepared 5 Nov 2026, the evening of the match. Source: Cricbuzz full
scorecard, with every innings total, dismissal and bowling figure
cross-checked against the CricTracker full scorecard (the two cards
agree on all 19 batting lines, all 13 bowling lines and both totals;
one press report's "127" for Janakpur is wrong on its own arithmetic
and is disregarded). Player of the Match: Rohit Paudel (CricTracker).
Toss: Janakpur Bolts won it and elected to field, as the post-toss
lock recorded (Janakpur 0.4931).

**Result:** Lumbini Lions won by 16 runs. Lumbini Lions 149/8 in 20
overs; Janakpur Bolts 133/8 in 20 overs, chasing 150. Lumbini go to
7 points (three wins and the shared point from the abandoned Match 7)
and stay top of the table; Janakpur fall to 2–3, their third defeat
in five matches.

## The one-line read

A match Janakpur lost in eleven balls in the middle of the chase.
The powerplay was honest — 40/1 against a 150 target, on profile —
and then Tilak Raj Bhandari and D'Arcy Short took three wickets for
three runs between overs 8.5 and 10.4, and the chase never recovered:
53/3 became 56/6, and Harmeet Singh and Aaditya Mahata's 43-run
eighth-wicket stand came with the asking rate already past 13 an
over. Lumbini's 149 was itself below their bat-first average, but
it was 17 more than Janakpur's chase average — and Janakpur made
exactly that average, 133.

## How Lumbini approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A below-average bat-first total that still won.** S1–S2:
  batting first Lumbini average 160.2 and win 33.3% of those games
  (6 games). Today, put in after losing the toss, they made 149/8
  — 11 below that average. The shape was familiar, though: a
  first-ball wicket (D'Arcy Short caught off Lalit Rajbanshi for a
  duck), a rebuild through the captain, and a late push. Rohit
  Paudel top-scored with 38 off 31 (1.23 runs per ball against a
  1.2434 prior — exactly on his prior) and Niroshan Dickwella made
  27 off 26 (1.04 against a 1.1893 prior, below it). Dilip Nath's
  21 off 15 (1.40 against a 1.1068 prior) was the one Lumbini
  innings above its prior by a distance; Aasif Sheikh 21 off 21
  (1.00 against 1.2998) and Shamim Hossain 13 off 13 (1.00 against
  1.3129) both scored well below theirs.
- **Powerplay and death both on profile.** S1–S2 batting in the
  powerplay Lumbini average 43.9 runs and lose 1.79 wickets per
  match (17 matches). Today they made 39/1. At the death they made
  32 runs from 117/5 in 17.2 overs to 149/8 — against a profile
  death return of 31.9 runs per match, on the number, driven by
  Ruben Trumpelmann's 15 off 8 (1.88 against a 1.3077 prior) and
  Sundeep Jora's 9 off 4 (2.25 against a 1.2375 prior).
- **The bowling won it in the middle overs, not the powerplay.**
  S1–S2 bowling in the powerplay Lumbini concede 39.1 runs and
  take 2.16 wickets per match. Today they conceded 40 and took
  only 1 (Parvez Hossain Emon lbw to Trumpelmann in the second
  over). The damage came later: Short 3/23 in 4 overs (economy
  5.75 against a 7.516 prior) and Tilak Raj Bhandari 2/5 in 2
  overs (economy 2.50 against a 6.196 prior) — the two of them
  took five wickets for 28 runs, and Tilak's two came in five
  balls (Anil Sah bowled 9.1, Bishal Shusling caught 9.5). Rohit
  Paudel's own 4-0-18-0 (economy 4.50 against a 6.912 prior)
  kept the chase strangled from the other end, and Sher Malla
  took 2/29 including Harmeet. Kishore Mahato was the one
  Lumbini bowler taken apart: 2-0-24-0 (economy 12.00 against a
  7.315 prior).

## How Janakpur approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A chase that finished on its average and still lost by 16.**
  S1–S2 chasing Janakpur average 132.4 and win 53.3% (15 games).
  Today they made 133/8 — the average, to the run — chasing 150.
  The powerplay was on profile (40/1 against 42.0 runs and 2.0
  wickets per match), and the death was on profile too (25 runs
  in the last 2.2 overs without losing a wicket, against a 26.8
  profile return). The match was lost between the two: overs 9
  to 13 produced 12 runs for four wickets.
- **The blueprint's opener did not open.** The season blueprint
  opens with Anil Sah. Today Trit Raj Das and Parvez Hossain Emon
  opened instead, and Anil batted at five and made 1 off 2 (0.50
  runs per ball against a 1.1731 prior). The openers combined for
  19 runs — Trit 18 off 18 (1.00 against a 1.1159 prior), Parvez
  1 off 5 (0.20 against a 1.2738 prior). Saif Hassan, the one
  batter in form after his 71 in Match 12, made 28 off 25 (1.12
  against a 1.1949 prior) and was third out at 53/3.
- **The middle order contributed 14 runs between four batters.**
  Anil Sah 1 (2), Bishal Shusling 4 (8), James Neesham 9 (11) and
  Abhisekh Tiwari 0 (4) — Neesham at 0.82 runs per ball against a
  1.302 prior, Abhisekh a duck against a 1.1263 prior. Bishal's
  bowling was the better half of his game, 4-0-21-2 (economy 5.25
  against a 7.04 debutant-default prior), taking Dickwella and
  Dilip Nath.
- **The lower order rescued the score, not the result.** Harmeet
  Singh 33 off 20 (1.65 against a 1.218 prior — the best batting
  return of the Janakpur innings against its prior) and Aaditya
  Mahata 30 not out off 24 (1.25 against a 1.2004 prior) added
  43 for the eighth wicket, from 65/7 to 108/8, with Mahata
  hitting four sixes. By the time Harmeet fell in the 18th over
  the asking rate was past 13 an over, and the stand only cut
  the margin. Harmeet also bowled 4-0-25-1 (economy 6.25
  against a 6.634 prior) and Rahul Yadav took 2/17 in 2 overs
  (economy 8.50 against a 7.04 default) — the wickets of Aasif
  Sheikh and Sundeep Jora. Saif Hassan bowled a maiden in his
  4-1-31-0, and Lalit Rajbanshi 4-0-31-1 (economy 7.75 against
  a 6.789 prior) took the first-ball wicket of Short.
- **Moeen Ali did not play.** He was named on the bench and did
  not bat or bowl, so the departure recorded in the availability
  log stands: the Moeen-less plan the Match 14 card wrote is
  now the reality, and the death bowling he used to own went to
  Harmeet, Bishal and Lalit. Ryan Burl, who appeared on one
  pre-match squad list, was not in the XI either. The XI, from

  Emon, Trit Raj Das, Anil Sah (wk), Saif Hassan, Bishal
  Shusling, James Neesham, Harmeet Singh (c), Abhisekh Tiwari,

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 21, Mon 10 Nov, 17:00)

1. **Settle the opening pair before Sudurpaschim, or repeat this
   chase.** Two different opening combinations in two matches
   (Match 12 opened with Moeen Ali; today Trit and Parvez) and
   neither gave the innings a platform. The blueprint's answer
   is Anil Sah opening — he has not opened since the blueprint
   was written, and his one ball at five today settles nothing.
   Five days to Match 21 is the time to pick a pair and stop
   experimenting.
2. **The middle order is now the problem, not the powerplay.**
   The powerplay has been on profile twice running; the collapse
   has come in overs 9–13 in both defeats. Neesham at six made 9
   off 11 today after 27 off 15 in Match 12 — the middle engine
   the blueprint puts at four or five is not firing there, and
   the four batters from four to seven made 14 runs between
   them today.
3. **Do not read 149 as a par-beating total.** Lumbini's 149 was
   below their own bat-first average of 160.2 and below the TU
   par band in the low 150s. Janakpur lost this match to their
   own chase, not to a big total — 150 was gettable, and the
   innings that made exactly the chase average still fell 17
   short because the wickets went in a cluster.
4. **Short is the wicket to plan for, again.** He took 3/23
   today, and the Match 14 card's warning about Lumbini's
   bowling was answered with wickets in the middle overs, from
   him and Tilak as much as from Trumpelmann.
   Sudurpaschim's attack is a different question, but the
   lesson travels: Janakpur's chase dies when three wickets
   fall in eleven balls, and the next opponent will have
   watched this one.
