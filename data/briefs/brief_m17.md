## The one-line read

A match Pokhara lost in the last four overs, not the first six: they
were ahead of Chitwan's pace at every checkpoint — 55/2 to 44/0 after
the powerplay, 103/5 to 91/4 after 15 overs — and then lost their
last five wickets for 18 runs while Chitwan's last five overs had
produced 60. Arjun Kumal's 45 was 37% of Pokhara's total, and nobody
else passed 19.

## How Chitwan approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first total two below their average, built backwards.**
  S1–S2 batting first Chitwan average 153.3 and win 50.0% of those
  games (10 games). Today, put in after losing the toss, they made
  151/6 — but the shape was inverted: a careful powerplay (44/0
  against a profile 38.9 runs and 2.0 wickets), a stalled middle
  (47/4 in overs 7–15, 91/4 at the 15-over mark), then 60/2 in the
  last five overs against a profile death return of 33.4 runs per
  match.
- **The openers did the quiet work, then three wickets fell for
  three runs.** Arjun Saud made 31 off 27 (1.15 runs per ball
  against a 1.1639 prior) and Dawid Malan 28 off 28 (1.00 against
  1.1702) in a 60-run opening stand; the first wicket did not fall
  until 8.6 overs. Then 60/0 became 63/3 in eleven balls — Dan
  Mousley made 2 off 5 (0.40 against a 1.0848 prior) and Kushal
  Malla 11 off 13 (0.85 against a 1.2309 prior) — and the innings
  needed rescuing.
- **The rescue was one partnership and one batter.** Ravi Bopara
  made 35 off 28 (1.25 against a 1.2554 prior, right on his number)
  and Sikandar Raza 37 off 16 (2.31 against a 1.2941 prior, two
  fours and four sixes) in a 60-run fifth-wicket stand, 86/4 to
  146/5. Bibek Yadav finished 0 not out off 1 and Kamal Singh
  Airee 4 not out off 2 (2.00 against a 1.1658 prior). Without the
  Raza–Bopara stand, Chitwan were heading for roughly 120.
- **The bowling was two-paced: three tight spells, one leak.**
  S1–S2 bowling in the powerplay Chitwan concede 42.4 runs and take
  2.18 wickets per match. Today they conceded 55 and took 2.
  Kamal Singh Airee took 2-1-7-3 (economy 3.50 against an 8.026
  prior), Kushal Malla 4-0-12-1 (3.00 against 7.185) and Rijan
  Dhakal 4-0-24-4 (6.00 against 7.478). Dan Mousley went 2-0-10-1
  (5.00 against 7.14) and Mousom Dhakal 2-0-11-1 (5.50 against
  7.234). The leak was Ranjit Kumar, 3-0-34-0 (11.33 against a
  6.831 prior), and Sikandar Raza's 2-0-19-0 (9.50 against 7.159)
  was the only other spell over seven an over. Five of Mousley's
  wides were among the 11 Pokhara conceded in extras — the only
  untidy note in an otherwise controlled defence.

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 21, Mon 10 Nov, 17:00 v Sudurpaschim; Match 25, Fri 13 Nov v Chitwan)

1. **Match 25 is now a direct table clash.** Chitwan sit one
   point ahead of Janakpur, 5 to 4, and it is Janakpur's last
   league game. A win takes Janakpur to 6 and past Chitwan; a
   loss leaves Janakpur on 4 with Chitwan on 7 and the playoff
   race decided without them. Everything between now and the
   13th is preparation for that one game.
2. **Chitwan's template is written down now: survive the
   middle, win the death, defend with two bowlers.** Their
   innings today was 44/0, then 47/4, then 60/2 — the damage
   came entirely in the last five overs, and almost all of it
   from Raza. Janakpur's death bowling needs a Raza plan before
   the 13th, the same way Match 16's brief called for a
   Ratnayake plan. And their attack has an identified soft
   over: Ranjit Kumar went at 11.33 today, the only Chitwan
   bowler over seven an over apart from Raza's part-time two.
   Janakpur's batting should target those overs and survive
   Airee (3.50) and Malla (3.00).
3. **The collapse cluster is now four matches running, and one
   of them was ours.** Match 14: Janakpur 53/3 became 56/6 in
   eleven balls. Match 15: Biratnagar 29/3 became 35/7. Match
   16: Karnali 26/3 inside five overs of a chase. Match 17:
   Pokhara 103/5 became 121 all out. The disease is the same in
   all four — not the powerplay itself, but the cluster of
   wickets that follows it. The three days to Sudurpaschim are
   for one thing above all: a plan for overs 7–13 that protects
   wickets instead of chasing the rate back.
4. **Pokhara are no longer a factor, and the one-batter lesson
   travels again.** Janakpur already beat Pokhara in Match 9
   (147/7 to 138/9) and do not face them again in the league;
   Pokhara finish the season pointless unless something changes
   radically. But Kumal's 45 — 37% of his side's total — losing
   by 30 runs is the same shape as Miller's 93 not out losing
   by 39 in Match 16: a batting order needs two contributors,
   not one hero.
5. **The lock's favourite lost, and the toss did not decide
   it.** Pokhara won the toss, fielded, and the model made them
   favourites at 0.527 — then they were bowled out for 121.
   Chasing at Kirtipur under lights is not the edge the toss
   suggested today; the team that batted first won the death
   overs 60–18.
