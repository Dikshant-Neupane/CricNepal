## The one-line read

A top order that fell for 7 runs in the first two overs — and still
won, because the captain anchored the rebuild and the chase died the
same way Janakpur's died in Match 21: early wickets, then a middle
that could not score fast enough.

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 25, Fri 13 Nov, 17:00 v Chitwan)

1. **Tonight is still a must-win, and it is the last league game.**
   A Janakpur win takes them to 6, level with Biratnagar, so the
   playoff race then turns on the remaining results and net run
   rate. Lose, and Janakpur finish on 4 points and the season ends
   in the league stage. Chitwan sit on 5 — a direct table clash
   with a side Janakpur can overtake — and Chitwan still have
   Match 27 v Karnali on Sunday, so tonight does not end their
   season the way it ends ours.
2. **The top of the table is settled.** Sudurpaschim finish on 10
   with their league stage complete; no result tonight changes who
   finishes first. The race is for the remaining playoff places.
3. **The chase lesson, twice over.** Kathmandu's chase died the same
   way Janakpur's died in Match 21 — early wickets, then a middle
   that could not score: 41 for 3, then 48 for 5, with one batter
   consuming 24 balls for 8. Janakpur's Match 21 chase died at 25
   for 3. Friday needs the rebuild pair the Match 25 card
   prescribes, not just a fast start: Anil Sah opens, and whoever
   opens with him must see off Rijan Dhakal and Ranjit Kumar
   rather than trade early wickets with them.
4. **A set batter batting through is the difference.** Airee's 60
   off 55 was below his own rate and still won the game, because
   he was there at the end. Chitwan's version of that batter is
   Ravi Bopara — 55 off 35 in Match 20, 171 runs in the tournament
   — and Kamil Mishara, whose death number (1.5433) punishes
   survival. Do not let either bat through; Harmeet Singh's
   powerplay economy (5.89 on our priors) goes at Mishara while
   the field is up.
5. **Three run-outs in one match.** Heazlett and Hemantha were both
   run out for Kathmandu, and Aarif Sheikh for Sudurpaschim.
   Calling between the wickets cost Kathmandu twice; the Match 21
   lesson (two run-outs in six balls) is still live for tonight.
6. **The favourite lost again — and the pre-season lines called it.**
   The P2 toss adjustment moved Kathmandu from 0.4773 to 0.5011
   and the chase failed; P1 (Sudurpaschim 0.5227) and the
   simulator (0.5225) had it right. Tonight's frozen line is a
   coin flip (Chitwan 0.5005, Janakpur 0.4995). The P1 post-XI lock
   tonight is the first number that prices the actual teams.
7. **The strip already has a day game on it.** Match 24 was played
   on the same ground hours before tonight's game, and it was
   low-scoring — 125 for 6, then 118 for 7. Tonight's par read
   should use the day game as evidence, not just the Day-Night
   table mean of 160.3: the toss read matters more than usual.
