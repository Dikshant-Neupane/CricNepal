## The one-line read

A five-over sprint, not a cricket match in the usual sense — and it must not be read as one. Biratnagar's first win of the season came from 30 balls of top-order power (Shehan Jayasuriya 39 off 11, Lokesh Bam 25 off 10, Andre Russell 29 not out off 10) and one genuinely controlled bowling spell (Russell 2-0-8-1) in a game where every other bowler went at 14 or worse. Sudurpaschim, the S1–S2 chase machine, never got going: their captain made 1 off 5, their finisher arrived too late, and 36 runs was the gap. Everything below carries the same caveat: six-to-eleven-ball innings do not re-rate a player.

## How Biratnagar approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first side that finally won — in a format that tests none of its habits.** S1–S2: batting first they average 151.9 and win 42.9% of those games; chasing they average 128.6 and win 44.4%. Today they batted first and won, but a 105 in 5 overs (21.00 an over) has no meaningful comparison to a 20-over average, and no powerplay or death phase exists in a five-over game. The shape, not the total, is the usable evidence: three batters, all scoring above 2.40 runs per ball, and no fourth batter needed.
- **Lokesh Bam is now the form batter of the tournament's first week, across three different games.** 25 off 10 (2.50 runs per ball against a 1.34 prior), after 26 off 14 against Chitwan in Match 2 and 87 off 52 against Karnali in Match 6. Three innings, three shapes — anchor, century-chase, sprint — and he has scored in all of them. Tuesday's card must price him as the innings, as the Match 6 brief already said.
- **Shehan Jayasuriya's 39 off 11 was the innings that set the game.** 3.55 runs per ball, two fours and five sixes, including 30 off the first over. No priors row exists for him — he is still absent from the player master, a gap flagged in the 30 Oct sweep — so there is no versus-prior read, and the 39 must be carried as a raw figure, not a rating.
- **Andre Russell played the only two-way game of the night.** 29 not out off 10 with the bat (2.90 against a 1.3321 prior) and 2-0-8-1 with the ball (4.00 an over against a 7.892 prior), dismissing Sherfane Rutherford first ball. In a game where the next-best economy was 14.00, Russell's two overs were the difference between 105 defended and 105 chased.
- **The rest of the bowling was expensive, not controlled.** Angelo Mathews 1-0-15-1 (15.00 against a 7.126 prior), Rupesh Singh 1-0-18-1 (no priors row) and Pratis GC 1-0-25-0 (no priors row). Mathews took the wicket that mattered — Airee for 1 — but at a sprint economy that would be a problem over four overs. Rupesh and Pratis are unknown quantities, not proven weaknesses: one over each in a five-over game proves neither.
- **The XI note for Tuesday's card.** Biratnagar's XI was Lokesh Bam (wk), Shehan Jayasuriya, Andre Russell, Imran Sheikh, Angelo Mathews, Narayan Joshi, Shubham Ranjane, Rupesh Singh, Sandeep Lamichhane (captain), Wasir Ahmed and Pratis GC. Charith Asalanka and Sandeep Lamichhane did not bowl; Asalanka did not appear on the scorecard at all despite featuring in pre-match XI lists — the scorecard, not the pre-match lists, is followed here (see Data notes).

## How Sudurpaschim approach games — and what changed tonight

- **A chase side that failed a chase, but the chase was five overs long.** S1–S2: Sudurpaschim chase at 134.0 on average and win 80.0% of their chases (5 games), and bat first at 156.2 with a 76.9% win rate across 13 games — the strongest record in the league. Tonight they chased 106 in 5 overs and made 70/3. The 80% chase record is a 20-over record; it did not survive contact with a sprint, and no 20-over conclusion should be drawn from its failure here.
- **The top order failed and the recovery arrived one over too late.** Sherfane Rutherford made 0 off 1 (0.00 against a 1.3546 prior), bowled by Russell. Dipendra Singh Airee, the captain, made 1 off 5 (0.20 against a 1.382 prior) — the opposite of his Match 4 finish (51 not out). Saif Zaib made 17 off 10 (1.70 against a 1.2587 prior under his priors name Saif Ali Zaib) — above himself, but not fast enough for a 106 chase. Aarif Sheikh made 17 not out off 8 (2.13 against a 1.1919 prior) and Scott Kuggeleijn 28 not out off 6 (4.67 against a 1.2011 prior, one four and four sixes) — the fastest scoring of the chase, but Kuggeleijn arrived at the fall of the third wicket with the required rate already past 20.
- **Their bowling had one wicket-taker and no control.** Abinash Bohara took both Biratnagar wickets, 2-0-28-2 (14.00 an over against a 7.669 prior) — expensive, but the only Sudurpaschim bowler to take a wicket. The rest went at 17.00 or worse: Scott Kuggeleijn 1-0-17-0 (17.00 against a 6.84 prior), Hemant Dhami 1-0-25-0 (25.00 against a 7.5 prior) and Tharindu Ratnayake 1-0-31-0 (31.00, no priors row), whose first over went for 31. In a five-over game, one 31-run over is a fifth of the innings.
- **Availability, for the record.** Sudurpaschim's XI was Sherfane Rutherford, Saif Zaib, Dipendra Singh Airee (captain), Aarif Sheikh, Scott Kuggeleijn, Binod Bhandari (wk), Tharindu Ratnayake, Ishan Pandey, Hemant Dhami, Anil Kharel and Abinash Bohara. ## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **Five-over games must not move 20-over plans.** The Match 9 Pokhara card (filed 30 Oct) and the season blueprint both rest on 20-over evidence — powerplay wickets, middle-overs spin, death matchups. Nothing tonight re-rates any of that: no bowler bowled more than two overs, no batter faced more than eleven balls, and the TU par tables (low 150s over 20 overs) have no five-over equivalent. The one transferable lesson is procedural, not tactical: on a rain day, the toss and the XI may not be published before the gate at all (as with both of today's fixtures), and a missing lock is the correct outcome, not a gap to fill late.
- **The captain-finisher pattern now has a counter-example, with a caveat.** The Match 6 brief named a league pattern: Sompal Kami finished a chase unbeaten, Dipendra Singh Airee finished one unbeaten in Match 4. Tonight Airee made 1 off 5 and Sudurpaschim lost. Five balls do not break a pattern — but they do say the pattern is not a law, and the 10 Nov card should plan for Airee failing early as well as finishing late.
- **Biratnagar's bowling shape for Tuesday is now a known unknown, not a weakness.** One proven control bowler in this format (Russell, 4.00 tonight against a 7.892 prior), one proven wicket-taker who is expensive (Mathews, 15.00 tonight), and two one-over unknowns (Rupesh, Pratis). Tuesday's card should target the unknown overs only if the XI repeats — and should not assume it will.
