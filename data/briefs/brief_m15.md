## The one-line read

A match Biratnagar lost in the powerplay and never got back. They
made 23/3 in the first six overs, lost four wickets for 6 runs,
29/3 becoming 35/7 between overs 7.5 and 9.4, and the only stand of
the innings was Angelo Mathews and Rupesh Singh's 39 off 40; once
it ended at 74/8 the last two wickets added 9 runs. Kathmandu
then chased 84 in 58 balls, losing one wicket, with David Warner
finishing 44 not out off 24.

- **A bat-first total 70 below their average.** S1–S2 batting
  first Biratnagar average 152.8 and win 50.0% of those games
  (10 games). Today, put in after losing the toss, they made
  83 all out — their eighth all-out in 21 matches (seven in 20
  S1–S2 matches), and a seventh sub-120 total after six in S1–S2.
  The shape was the familiar failure mode, not a new one: the
  top order went cheaply and the middle could not rebuild.
- **The powerplay was half a powerplay.** S1–S2 batting in the
  powerplay Biratnagar average 46.0 runs and lose 1.95 wickets
  per match (20 matches). Today they made 23/3 — half the runs,
  a wicket more than average. Shehan Jayasuriya made 5 off 8
  (0.63 runs per ball against a 1.113 prior), Lokesh Bam 6 off
  10 (0.60 against 1.34), Imran Sheikh a 3-ball duck (against
  a 1.1555 prior) and Basir Ahamad 10 off 14 (0.71 against

## How Kathmandu approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A chase on profile, finished at twice the required rate.**
  S1–S2 chasing Kathmandu average 136.8 and win 75.0% (8 games).
  Today they chased 84 and made 86/1 in 9.4 overs. The powerplay
  produced 48/1 against a profile 38.9 runs and 2.53 wickets per
  match — more runs, fewer wickets lost. David Warner finished
  44 not out off 24 (1.83 runs per ball against a 1.2504 prior,
  two fours and three sixes) and William Bosisto 23 not out off
  24 (0.96 runs per ball; no priors row in the frozen set). Their
  unbroken second-wicket stand of 69 off 43 balls ended the game
  with 62 balls to spare.
- **Sahil Patel opened and kept wicket.** He made 10 off 11
  (0.91 against a 1.1352 prior) before Aprajit Poudel bowled
  him. It is the first match since K. S. Bharat's withdrawal
  removed him from the squad on 5 November, and the pinned
  Kathmandu XI change — Sahil in at wicketkeeper — played out
  exactly as the corrected data had it: he opened, kept wicket,
  and was the only Kathmandu wicket to fall.
- **The bowling was better than its profile on both counts.**
  S1–S2 bowling in the powerplay Kathmandu concede 40.1 runs and
  take 2.06 wickets per match. Today they conceded 23 and took
  3. Santosh Yadav took 3/11 in 3 overs (economy 3.67 against a
  6.926 prior) and was Player of the Match; Jahandad Khan took
  2/19 in 3.3 overs (economy 5.43 against an 8.015 prior) and
  held three catches — Lokesh Bam, Narayan Joshi and Andre
  Russell, all off his own team's bowling. Karan KC took 4-0-17-1
  (economy 4.25 against a 7.818 prior), Shahab Alam 4-0-13-1
  (economy 3.25 against a 6.513 prior) and Sahan Arachchige
  2-0-7-2 (economy 3.50 against a 7.149 prior). Dev Shah's
  2-0-15-1 (economy 7.50 against the 7.04 default) was the one
  spell that went at more than a run a ball.
- **Batting depth was never used.** Eight Kathmandu batters did
  not bat — Rashid Khan, Bhim Sharki, Sahan Arachchige, Jahandad
  Khan, Karan KC, Santosh Yadav, Shahab Alam and Dev Shah. A
  chase won by the top three alone is a comfortable win, not a
  test of the middle order, and it says nothing about how
  Kathmandu bat under pressure.

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 21, Mon 10 Nov, 17:00)

1. **The collapse pattern is now two matches running, and one of
   them was ours.** Match 14: Janakpur 53/3 became 56/6 in eleven
   balls. Match 15: Biratnagar 29/3 became 35/7, four wickets for
   6 runs between overs 7.5 and 9.4.
   Both chases and both first innings died the same way — not in
   the powerplay, but in the cluster of wickets that follows it.
   The five days to Sudurpaschim are for one thing above all:
   a plan for overs 7–13 that protects wickets instead of
   chasing the rate back.
2. **The table is a five-way tie at 4 points, and net run rate
   may decide the last playoff places.** Kathmandu's 62 balls to
   spare is the kind of margin that moves NRR a long way in one
   evening; Janakpur's two remaining league games (Sudurpaschim
   on 10 November, Chitwan on 13 November) are both must-win in
   practice, and a win with balls to spare is worth materially
   more than a win off the last ball.
3. **Russell's departure reshapes the playoff race, not our
   fixtures.** He played his last match today — 3 off 5 and
   1-0-14-0 — and leaves for the Abu Dhabi T10. Janakpur do not
   face Biratnagar again in the league, but Biratnagar's
   remaining games get harder without their marquee all-rounder,
   and a weakened Biratnagar is one fewer rival for the fourth
   playoff place.
4. **Warner is in form, and he is a playoff problem, not a league
   one.** His 44 not out off 24 followed a quiet start to the
   season, and Janakpur already banked their win over Kathmandu
   in Match 5. Whoever meets Kathmandu in the playoffs meets a
   Warner who has found his timing — and a Kathmandu attack that
   today took 10 wickets for 83 without its captain conceding
   more than 4.25 an over.
5. **The corrected data keeps checking out.** Sahil Patel opening
   and keeping wicket for Kathmandu is the second pinned-XI
   change from the 4 November corrections to play out exactly as
   filed. The model path that produced the Match 14 and 15 locks
   is the landed one, and nothing in this match contradicts it.
