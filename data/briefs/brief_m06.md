Prepared 29 Oct 2026, the night of the match. **Result:** Karnali Yaks won by 4 wickets (1 ball remaining). Biratnagar Kings 186/7 in 20 overs; Karnali 187/6 in 19.5 overs, chasing 187. Toss: Karnali, fielded. Player of the Match: Sompal Kami (62 not out off 32, and 4/33).

## The one-line read

The highest total of the season so far was chased down with a ball to spare, by a captain who did both jobs: Sompal Kami took 4/33, including the innings' two biggest wickets, and then finished the chase unbeaten on 62. Biratnagar's 186 was not a par score defended badly — it was 39 runs above the TU first-innings mean of 147.5 and still lost, because Karnali's sixth- and seventh-wicket pairs added 66 and 64 unbroken, and Biratnagar's death bowling had no answer to the second of them.

## How Biratnagar approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first side that finally batted to its ceiling — and still lost.** S1–S2: batting first they average 151.9 and win 42.9% of those games. Today they made 186, their best first-innings shape of the season, and lost. The lesson is not that 186 is unsafe in general; it is that this Biratnagar attack, on this night, could not defend a total 34 runs above its own bat-first average.
- **Lokesh Bam is now the form batter of the tournament's first week.** 87 off 52 (1.67 runs per ball against a 1.34 prior), with three fours and nine sixes, after 26 off 14 against Chitwan in Match 2. He reached fifty before the drinks break, added 67 for the fourth wicket with Angelo Mathews, and was dismissed in the 16th over with Biratnagar at 144/4. He fell 13 short of a century, but the innings was his: no other Biratnagar batter passed 31.
- **The rest of the batting was support, not a second threat.** Mathews made 31 off 23 (1.35 against a 1.1399 prior — above himself) and Andre Russell 27 off 15 (1.80 against a 1.3321 prior). Basir Ahamad made 13 off 7 (1.86 against a 1.2234 prior). The failures were at the top: Imran Sheikh 5 off 7 (0.71 against a 1.1555 prior) fell in the third over, and Charith Asalanka 3 off 6 (0.50 against a 1.2237 prior) fell in the sixth, leaving Biratnagar 39/2. Shehan Jayasuriya's 11 off 10 ended in a run out; no priors row exists for him, so there is no versus-prior read.
- **Their bowling had one control bowler and no death answer.** Mathews was the exception: 4-0-25-3 (6.25 an over against a 7.126 prior), taking the first three Karnali wickets. Everyone else went at 8.00 or worse: Charith Asalanka 4-0-32-1 (8.00 against 7.196), Naren Saud 2-0-16-0 (8.00 against 6.896), Sandeep Lamichhane 3.5-0-35-1 (9.13 against 6.461), Basir Ahamad 1-0-11-0 (11.00 against 7.087), Andre Russell 2-0-24-0 (12.00 against 7.892), and Pratish GC 3-0-39-1 (13.00 against 7.591). Karnali needed eight off the final over and got them with a ball to spare, after a dropped catch offered by Gulshan Jha in that over.
- **The XI note for Tuesday's card.** Biratnagar's XI was Imran Sheikh, Lokesh Bam, Charith Asalanka, Shehan Jayasuriya, Angelo Mathews, Andre Russell, Basir Ahamad, Sujan Thapaliya, Pratish GC, Sandeep Lamichhane (captain) and Naren Saud. Shubham Ranjane fielded as a substitute (he took Pawan Sarraf's catch) but did not bat or bowl.

## How Karnali approach games — and what changed tonight

- **A chase side that chased a total above its own history.** S1–S2: Karnali chase at 132.0 on average and win 60.0% of their chases (5 games). Tonight they chased 187 — 55 runs above that average — and are now 2–0 for the season after beating Janakpur by 19 runs in Match 3. Two wins, two different shapes: defending 132 against the Bolts, chasing 187 tonight.
- **The top order failed and the captain fixed it twice.** Mark Watt made 1 off 7 (0.14 against a 1.3136 prior), Hussain Talat 0 off 4 (0.00 against a 1.1289 prior), and Akbar Ali 1 off 4 (0.25 against a 1.1027 prior). The recovery came in two partnerships: Sompal Kami and Pawan Sarraf added 66 for the sixth wicket (Sarraf 36 off 28, 1.29 against a 1.1948 prior — himself), then Kami and Gulshan Jha added 64 unbroken for the seventh (Jha 28 off 21, 1.33 against a 1.319 prior — exactly himself). Kami's 62 off 32 (1.94 against a 1.2291 prior) is the highest individual score against a prior in this match by a wide margin.
- **The other two bright batting lines were short and sharp.** Ariful Islam made 32 off 12 (2.67 against a 1.1102 prior) and Deepak Dumre 20 off 11 (1.82 against a 1.1561 prior). Twelve and eleven balls are not a new profile for either man, but both innings kept the required rate within reach while wickets fell around them.
- **Kami's bowling was the other half of the award.** 4/33 in four overs (8.25 an over against a 7.675 prior — slightly above his base rate, but with the wickets that mattered): he dismissed Asalanka, Mathews, Bam and Russell, and caught-and-bowled Bam himself. Mark Watt was the control at the other end: 4-1-23-1 (5.75 against a 7.542 prior), including Imran Sheikh in the third over. The expensive Karnali bowlers were Nandan Yadav 4-0-43-1 (10.75 against 7.571), Yuvraj Khatri 2-0-18-0 (9.00 against 7.462), Unish Thakuri 3-0-26-0 (8.67 against 7.051) and Hussain Talat 3-0-39-0 (13.00 against 7.369) — Talat's night with the ball was as poor as his night with the bat.
- **Availability, for the record.** Karnali's XI was Mark Watt, Ariful Islam, Deepak Dumre, Hussain Talat, Pawan Sarraf, Akbar Ali, Sompal Kami (captain), Gulshan Jha, Nandan Yadav, Unish Thakuri and Yuvraj Khatri. Talat has now played both Karnali matches, so the PCB NOC doubt that surrounded him before Match 3 did not keep him out. David Miller and Sahibzada Farhan again did not play; neither has an arrival or return report in the sources read for this match.

## The mirror: what this means for Janakpur's own approach

- **186 chased is a warning against reading the TU par as a safety line.** The par tables say low 150s; the season's six matches have now produced totals of 87, 157, 132, 145, 115 and 186 batting first, and the 186 was chased. Wickets, not totals, decided it again: Biratnagar took six wickets but never took Kami, and the two partnerships that beat them were the sixth and seventh wickets. A side that bowls a team out, as Janakpur did to Kathmandu in Match 5, does not face this problem; a side that leaves a set finisher in, does.
- **The captain-finisher is now a league pattern, not a one-off.** Sompal Kami finished a chase unbeaten tonight; Dipendra Singh Airee finished one unbeaten in Match 4 (51 not out). The Bolts' own death plan, and the Match 9 and Match 12 cards, need a named answer to the opposition captain or set finisher — not just a death bowler, but a matchup for the man who is still there at over 17.
- **Biratnagar's bowling shape is now readable for Tuesday.** One control bowler (Mathews, 3 wickets at 6.25 tonight), a captain whose economy blew out tonight (Sandeep, 9.13 against a 6.461 prior), and two expensive overs-eaters (Pratish GC and Russell, 13.00 and 12.00). That is a different attack from the one that lost Match 2 by 8 runs chasing — tonight it was the bowling, not the batting, that lost the game.

## What this changes for the Bolts

**Match 9 v Pokhara Avengers (Sun 1 Nov, 17:00):** no direct read from tonight, but the general lesson carries: Pokhara made 145 batting first in Match 4 and lost it with 19 balls to spare. A total in the 140s is not a defence at TU unless wickets fall through the middle — Janakpur's Match 5 shape (bowling a side out for 115) is the template, and tonight is the counter-example of what happens when the finisher survives.

**Match 12 v Biratnagar Kings (Tue 3 Nov, 12:30):**
1. Plan for Bam first, Mathews second. Bam has now made 26 and 87 in his two S3 innings; Mathews has made runs (31 tonight) and taken the wickets (3/25 tonight) in the same game. The Tuesday card must price Bam as the innings, not as one of several threats.
2. Aim the batting at Pratish GC and Russell with the ball. Tonight they went at 13.00 and 12.00 an over; Sandeep's 9.13 is a one-game figure against a 6.461 prior, so treat him as the bowler to respect, not to target, until a second expensive game confirms it.
3. Do not let the game reach a set finisher at over 17. Biratnagar left Kami in and lost a game they led by 186 runs' worth of batting. The death plan for Tuesday must include a specific Kami-style answer — who bowls to the set batter, and who fields where — because Biratnagar's own death bowling tonight shows what happens without one.
4. Note Biratnagar are now 0–2, with a loss chasing (Match 2, by 8 runs) and a loss defending 186. Tuesday is their third game; a side chasing its first win after two different kinds of defeat is not a side to assume will fold, but it is a side whose bowling, not its batting, is the current weakness.

**Karnali Yaks:** no rematch in the league fixtures — the Bolts' only meeting with Karnali was Match 3, which Karnali won by 19 runs. Tonight's result matters for the table and for the pattern above, not for a future Karnali card.
