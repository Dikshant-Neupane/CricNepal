## The one-line read

A match Karnali lost twice: first when their bowlers let Sudurpaschim's
openers put on 68 without loss, then when their chase fell to 26/3
inside five overs. David Miller's unbeaten 93 off 55 — 68% of Karnali's
total — was a lone hand in both senses: the other eight Karnali batters
made 44 runs between them, and no other batter passed 18.

## How Sudurpaschim approach games (S1–S2 evidence, tested today)

- **A bat-first total 25 above their average.** S1–S2 batting first
  Sudurpaschim average 151.3 and win 71.4% of those games (14 games).
  Today, put in after losing the toss, they made 176/6 — their first
  bat-first innings of the season, built on the opening stand
  rather than a late surge.
- **The powerplay was a free hit.** S1–S2 batting in the powerplay
  Sudurpaschim average 43.7 runs and lose 1.73 wickets per match
  (22 matches). Today they made 63/0 — nineteen runs above profile
  and no wicket lost. Saif Zaib made 35 off 24 (1.46 runs per ball
  against a 1.2587 prior) and Binod Bhandari 27 off 22 (1.23 against
  1.148), and the first wicket did not fall until 68 in the eighth
  over. Karnali's powerplay bowling profile concedes 37.7 and takes
  2.05 wickets per match; today it conceded 63 and took none.
- **The middle order stalled, then the finishers fired.** Brandon
  McMullen made 24 off 16 (1.50 against a 1.2816 prior) and Ishan
  Pandey 23 off 13 (1.77 against a 1.1094 prior), but Dipendra Singh
  Airee made 10 off 14 (0.71 against a 1.382 prior) and Aarif Sheikh
  11 off 14 (0.79 against a 1.1919 prior). The innings was rescued
  at the death: from 136/5 at 16.2 overs Sudurpaschim added 40 runs
  in the last 3.4 overs, against a profile death return of 29.9 runs
  per match. Tharindu Ratnayake finished 22 not out off 12 (1.83
  against a 1.2156 prior) and Scott Kuggeleijn 13 not out off 6

- **The bowling was tight everywhere except two overs.** S1–S2

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts (Match 21, Mon 10 Nov, 17:00 v Sudurpaschim)

1. **The opening stand is the game.** Sudurpaschim's openers put on
   68 today and both scored above prior; across S1–S2 they lose 1.73
   wickets in the powerplay on average, and today they lost none.
   Janakpur's powerplay bowling has to break the stand early — the
   head-to-head is 1–3 against Sudurpaschim across four meetings, and
   letting Zaib and Bhandari bat through the powerplay is how those
   defeats have happened.
2. **The collapse pattern is now three matches running, and one of
   them was ours.** Match 14: Janakpur 53/3 became 56/6 in eleven
   balls. Match 15: Biratnagar 29/3 became 35/7. Match 16: Karnali
   26/3 inside five overs of a chase. The disease is the same in all
   three — not the powerplay itself, but the cluster of wickets that
   follows it. The four days to Sudurpaschim are for one thing above
   all: a plan for overs 7–13 that protects wickets instead of
   chasing the rate back.
3. **Sudurpaschim's death batting has a second gear now.** Ratnayake
   and Kuggeleijn added 40 in the last 3.4 overs at better than 1.8
   runs per ball each, against a profile death return of 29.9.
   Janakpur's death bowling needs a plan for Ratnayake in particular
   — he also took 1/18 at 4.50 an over, so he hurts both ways.
4. **Their attack has a soft underbelly, and it is identifiable.**
   Four Sudurpaschim bowlers went at 4.67 or better today, but
   Kuggeleijn went at 9.75 and Pandey at 14.00. Janakpur's batting
   should target those overs and survive the tight ones — the
   opposite of the Match 14 approach, where the middle order chased
   the rate against the wrong bowlers.
5. **Match 21 is effectively a playoff six-pointer.** Sudurpaschim
   sit on 6 points, Janakpur on 4. A win draws Janakpur level with
   one league game left (Chitwan, 13 November); a loss leaves them
   two points adrift of second place and needing favours elsewhere.
   Net run rate may still decide the last playoff places, so a win
   with balls to spare is worth materially more than a win off the
   last ball.
6. **The Miller lesson travels, even though Miller does not.**
   Janakpur do not face Karnali again in the league, but the shape
   of this defeat is a warning for any side that leans on one
   batter: 93 not out still lost by 39 runs because the other eight
   batters made 44 between them. A batting order needs two
   contributors, not one hero.
