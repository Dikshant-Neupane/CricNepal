## The one-line read

A must-win game lost in the first six overs — 22 for 3 in the
powerplay, 22 for 4 one ball later — and the chase that followed
proved the total was never enough, even after Janakpur's bowlers took
five wickets in Chitwan's own powerplay.

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts

1. **The season ends in the league stage.** The must-win is lost,
   and there is no next fixture: Janakpur finish on 4 points, two
   wins from six. Chitwan go to 7 and still have Match 27 v Karnali
   on Sunday — they must win that to keep their own playoff hopes
   alive, so tonight does not end their season the way it ends ours.
2. **The toss decision went against profile and against the result.**
   S1–S2 Janakpur field first in 72.7% of the tosses they win, and
   bat-first they win 33.3% of games. They won the toss and batted,
   and lost. The P2 lock still had Chitwan favourite at 0.5243 after
   the toss, and the simulator's read — Janakpur ahead, 149.7 plays
   136.7 — died with the powerplay.
3. **The prescribed XI did not happen.** The Match 25 card had Anil
   Sah opening; instead Trit Raj Das opened with Santosh Karki,
   playing his first match of the tournament, and Anil Sah was not
   in the XI at all. Saif Hassan was not in the XI either,
   consistent with the departure reports logged this week. The
   rebuild pair the card prescribed never got to bat together.
4. **The collapse pattern, third time.** Janakpur's Match 21 chase
   died at 25 for 3; tonight the innings died at 22 for 4 in the
   powerplay and 45 for 6 by the 12th over. The failure is the
   middle's scoring rate — Mahata 4 off 26, Neesham 0 off 8 — not
   the top order alone: only Shusling among the top six batted at
   his prior.
5. **The bowling is not the problem.** Rahul Yadav's 3 for 19 and
   Rajbanshi's 2 for 23 took five wickets in Chitwan's powerplay,
   and four of the six bowlers went under 5.50 an over. The defence
   of 88 was lost when the batting set it, not when the bowling
   bowled it.
