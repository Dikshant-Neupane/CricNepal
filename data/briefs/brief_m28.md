## How Pokhara approached it

Batting first against their profile. Pokhara's bat-first average
across the profile's eight matches is 140.9; tonight they made
224 — 83 above it, the highest team total in NPL history (the
previous record was Biratnagar's 220 against Pokhara last season).
The powerplay went 65 for 1, against a profile of 44.9 runs and
1.88 wickets per match. The innings was one stand: Yuvraj Samra
and Kushal Bhurtel added 186 for the second wicket, the highest
partnership for any wicket in NPL history, and the pair were only
separated in the 19th over — Bhurtel for 99, Samra for 89, five
balls apart. Chamika Karunaratne's unbeaten 19 off 7 took Pokhara
from 208 for 3 to 224. With the ball they defended 225 the way a
side with nothing to lose should: Dinesh Kharel 4 for 18 and Bipin
Khatri 3 for 28 shared seven wickets, and Sunny BK bowled a
maiden-wicket over (1-1-0-1). Kathmandu never had a stand longer
than the 60 Patel and Bosisto put on for the second wicket.

## How Kathmandu approached it

Kathmandu won the toss, fielded as usual, and conceded the record
total. Their powerplay bowling conceded 65, against a profile of
40.1 runs per match, and every Kathmandu bowler with a prior went
above his prior economy: Rashid Khan 0 for 55 (13.75, prior 7.62),
Sahan Arachchige 0 for 40 in 3 overs (13.33, prior 7.15), Jahandad
Khan 1 for 47 (11.75, prior 8.02), Santosh Yadav 0 for 21 in 2
overs (10.50, prior 6.93), Karan KC 2 for 34 (8.50, prior 7.82).
Only KC took wickets in the first 18 overs, both of them the set
openers. The chase started on profile — 56 for 1 in the
powerplay, against 38.9 runs per match — then fell apart in the
middle: 72 for 2, 86 for 4, 137 for 7, 169 all out. Kathmandu's
chase average is 136.8, so 169 is above their usual chase score
and still 56 short; their chase win rate was 75% (6 of 8) and
falls to 6 of 9. The profile already records them all out seven
times in 17 matches; tonight adds another.

## Players against their priors

Bhurtel's 99 off 51 (1.94 runs per ball against a 1.46 prior) is
the highest score by a Nepali batter in NPL history, one run short
of the first Nepali NPL century; Samra's 89 off 55 (1.62 against
1.45) takes his season tally to 294, from 205 before the match.
Karunaratne's unbeaten 19 off 7 (2.71 against 1.21) finished the
innings. Kharel's 4 for 18 (economy 4.50 against a 7.18 prior) is
the bowling performance of the night — his four were Patel
(stumped), Bosisto, Arachchige and Karan KC, the top and middle of
the chase. Khatri's 3 for 28 (7.00 against 6.77) is dead on his
prior. Sunny BK, on a debutant-default profile, bowled a maiden
and took Sumit Maharjan; Ramesh Mendis, also on a debutant
default, went for 40 in 4 overs (10.00 against 7.04) but took
Jahandad at 130 for 5, ending the 44-run stand that was
Kathmandu's last recognised pair. For Kathmandu the runs came from
the wrong end of the order: Sahil Patel 34 off 23 (1.48 against
1.14), Rashid Khan 31 off 20 (1.55 against 1.04), Jahandad Khan 24
off 12 (2.00 against 1.21) and Sonu Devkota 29 off 14 (2.07
against a debutant-default 1.17). Bosisto 26 off 22 (1.18 against
1.17) and Bhim Sharki 8 off 7 (1.14 against 1.19) were dead on
their priors; nobody in Kathmandu's top four beat theirs by more
than Patel did.

## What it means for the Bolts

Nothing to prepare for — the Bolts' season is over, eliminated on
4 points in seventh, and there are no fixtures left. The closing
note is this: both of tonight's sides lost to the Bolts in the
league stage (M05: the Bolts beat Kathmandu; M09: the Bolts beat
Pokhara by 9 runs), yet Kathmandu go to the Eliminator and Pokhara
finish last. The playoff gap was never tonight's pair; it was the
four sides above them. Two things from tonight are worth carrying
into the review. First, Pokhara's ceiling: a side the Bolts beat
posted 224, 83 above its own bat-first average, on the back of one
186-run stand — the Bolts' win over them came on a day that
ceiling stayed shut, and the margin for error against a pair like
Samra and Bhurtel is one over. Second, Kathmandu's shape: a 56
for 1 powerplay followed by a middle-order collapse to 169 all out
is the same fragility the Bolts exploited in M05, and it is what
Chitwan will be planning for in Wednesday's Eliminator.
