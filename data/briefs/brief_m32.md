**Result:** Sudurpaschim Royals won by 10 wickets, with 39 balls to

## How Lumbini approached it

Sent in after losing the toss — the opposite of their preference:
they have fielded first in 90% of the tosses they have won. Their
bat-first average across the profile's six bat-first matches is
160.2, with a 33.3% win rate; tonight they made 155, five below
that average, and it was never enough on a night their bowling
took no wickets at all.

The start was slow rather than catastrophic. Aasif Sheikh fell lbw
to Kuggeleijn for 1 off 4 and Lumbini were 5 for 1 at 2.2 overs;
D'Arcy Short followed, bowled by Airee for 21 off 18, and they were
35 for 2 at 5.3. The powerplay produced 37 runs for two wickets,
per myRepublica. Niroshan Dickwella kept the rate up — 28 off 17 —
before being bowled by Ratnayake, and Lumbini were 74 for 3 at 10.1.
Captain Rohit Paudel made 30 off 31 and was run out at 108 for 4
in the 13.5th over; Sundeep Jora went for 4 off 5 and they were 118
for 5 at 14.3. Dilip Nath held one end through all of it, 39 not
out off 31 with three sixes, and Ali Sheikh provided the late
acceleration, 24 off 12, before falling at 150 for 6 in the 19.4th
over. Ruben Trumpelmann finished 4 not out off 2. The innings
leaked only four extras, so the shortfall was batting, not
discipline: nobody passed 39, and the top three managed 52 runs
between them.

## How Sudurpaschim approached it

A chase, which is their stronger suit: eight chases in the profile
at an average of 127.4 and a 75% win rate — but the target of 156
was nearly thirty above that average, and they made it look easy.
They reached 50 in 4.6 overs, scored 60 without loss in the
powerplay against a profile average of 43.7 for 1.73 wickets, were
90 at the 10-over mark, crossed 100 in 10.4 overs and 150 in 13.2,
per myRepublica. No Lumbini bowler took a wicket, and none went at
under 9.71 an over.

George Munsey decided it alone. His 125 not out off 54 — eight
fours, twelve sixes — came at 2.31 runs per ball against a prior of
1.4104, far above his number, and it is the highest individual
score of the season, passing Kamil Mishara's 120 not out in the
Eliminator three days earlier. It is also a complete reversal of his
own playoffs: he had made 8 off 15 in Qualifier 2 two days ago,
and 8 off 16 balls across the playoffs before tonight, the exact
form the morning preview held against him. Binod Bhandari held the
other end, 30 not out off 27 at 1.11 against a prior of 1.148 —
on his number, and exactly the supporting role the chase needed.
Sudurpaschim's bowling had set it up: Kuggeleijn 4-0-25-2, Dhami
4-0-23-1, Ratnayake 4-0-24-1 and Airee 4-0-25-1, all four going at
6.25 or better, with the expensive overs coming from the fifth and
sixth bowlers (Kharel 2-0-25-0, Pandey 2-0-30-0).

## What it means for the Janakpur Bolts

Nothing in the fixture sense: the Bolts were eliminated on 13 Nov,
when Chitwan beat them in the must-win Match 25, and they have no
matches left. The season is over, and this is the last results run
of it. What the Final leaves for the project and for next season:

- The toss decided the shape of the game. Sudurpaschim won it,
  fielded, and chased — the 17:00-slot pattern (chasing sides won
  55% of Season 3 matches in the slot) held, and the side batting
  first lost despite posting 155, above the 150 venue pivot that
  had carried a ~77% win rate. A total above the pivot is not a
  defence when the chase is wicketless.
- The morning's three-point refined adjustment to Lumbini was the
  error, not the model: the locked base line's 50.1% for
  Sudurpaschim was the honest number, and the post-toss line's
  52.5% was the best of the three calls. The preview's mechanism —
  Lumbini bowl first and strangle Sudurpaschim, as in both previous
  meetings — inverted entirely once Sudurpaschim won the toss, and
  its discount of Munsey (8 off 16 balls in the playoffs) was
  answered by the highest score of the season.
- For the Bolts' own planning: a playoff replacement overseas
  batter decided the final, and the team that kept wickets in hand
  through the chase never looked troubled. The lesson of the
  season's last game is the same as its first — wickets in hand
  beat runs on the board — and Lumbini, who used that mechanism to
  beat Sudurpaschim twice, were on the receiving end of it when it
  mattered most.
