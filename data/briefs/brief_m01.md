# Match 1 prep brief — what Lumbini v Sudurpaschim tells the Janakpur Bolts

Prepared 26 Oct 2026, the night of the match. Sources: ESPNcricinfo full scorecard (Match 1, verified), CricNepal ball-by-ball dataset for NPL Seasons 1–2 (64 matches, TU Kirtipur), player priors file.

**Result:** Lumbini Lions won by 7 wickets (61 balls remaining). Sudurpaschim 87 all out in 17.2 overs; Lumbini 88/3 in 9.5 overs. Toss: Lumbini, fielded. Player of the Match: Sher Malla.

## The one-line read

This was not a bad pitch and not an upset of a weak team. It was Lumbini executing the most repeatable winning pattern at this ground — field first, take powerplay wickets — against the strongest side of the first two seasons, whose one off-pattern night (4 powerplay wickets lost) ended the match by the sixth over.

## How Lumbini approach games (S1–S2 evidence, confirmed tonight)

- **Chase-first identity.** In S1–S2 they won the toss 8 times and fielded 7. Their win rate chasing is 55%, against 33% batting first. Tonight was the template again: field, strangle, chase at leisure.
- **Powerplay bowling is their weapon.** S1–S2: 2.00 powerplay wickets per match, 3+ in 6 of 17 matches, conceding 40.1 runs per powerplay. Tonight: 4 wickets inside the powerplay (Mahato and Malla), Sudurpaschim 34/4 after 5.4 overs.
- **They are the only side that does this to Sudurpaschim.** Sudurpaschim's innings against Lumbini across three seasons: 187, 145, 85 (S2 final), 87 (tonight). Nobody else bowls them out cheaply; Lumbini have now done it twice in a row.
- **Chase engine is the opening pair.** Tonight D'Arcy Short (40 off 18, 2.22 runs/ball against a 1.168 prior) and Aasif Sheikh (32 off 25, matching his elite 1.300 prior) put on 71 in 6.2 overs. Both their wickets fell to Anil Kharel only after the game was over as a contest.

## How Sudurpaschim approach games — and where tonight broke

- **Historically the league's benchmark:** 14 wins from 18 in S1–S2, averaging 156 batting first with a 77% win rate, and the league's best powerplay bowling (2.56 wickets per match, 3+ in 11 of 18).
- **Their batting is normally powerplay-safe:** 1.50 wickets lost per powerplay, the best of the three sides profiled here. Tonight they lost 4 — completely off-pattern, and with no recovery mechanism behind it: their three highest-prior batters (Airee 1.382, Rutherford 1.355, McMullen 1.282 runs/ball) returned 1.17, 0.25 and 1.00.
- **Read for 10 Nov:** Sudurpaschim are not suddenly weak. They are beatable in exactly one way — early wickets. If their top order survives the powerplay, their S1–S2 base rates (156 batting first, strong death batting at 33.9 runs per match in overs 16–20) take over.

## The mirror: Janakpur's own approach since Season 1

- **A chasing side, like Lumbini — but leakier.** Janakpur win 58% chasing and only 20% batting first (average 136). The toss plan writes itself: field first where possible.
- **Powerplay batting is Janakpur's biggest structural weakness.** 2.06 wickets lost per powerplay — the worst rate of the three sides — with 3+ lost in 6 of 17 matches. Tonight's match is the warning: at TU, losing the powerplay loses the game. The blueprint already answers this (Anil Sah opens, Moeen Ali never opens); Match 1 is the evidence for why it must hold.
- **Powerplay bowling is the gap Harmeet Singh exists to close.** Janakpur take 1.00 powerplay wicket per match and have never taken 3+ in the powerplay in two seasons. Lumbini take 2.00, Sudurpaschim 2.56. The Bolts' whole improvement case this season sits in this one phase.
- **Death batting is also the weakest of the three** (25.2 runs per match in overs 16–20, against Lumbini 31.9 and Sudurpaschim 33.9) — which is why the blueprint holds Moeen for overs 17–20 rather than spending him early.
- **Head-to-head history:** Janakpur 1–1 v Lumbini (won by 1 run in S1, lost by 4 wickets in S2); 1–3 v Sudurpaschim, including being bowled out for 51 in S1 — the franchise's lowest total. Respect both fixtures; neither is a banker.

## What this changes for the Bolts
